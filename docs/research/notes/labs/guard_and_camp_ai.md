# Guard and camp AI for stealth-action games: architectures, beliefs, coordination, communication, adaptation and navigation

Scope: prior art for Sightline's Lab E (camp brain) and the AI side of Lab A. Each finding is tagged **[shipped: game, year]** or **[research-only, year]**, and with **[secondary]** when the source is a journalist, analyst or course copy rather than the developer, or **[low confidence]** when it comes from a fan wiki, strategy guide, review or forum. Fit notes for Sightline (deterministic 30 Hz TypeScript simulation, plain-data `sim.state`, per-entity random streams, brains that see only their `GuardMind`) are in each question's Inferences section, one line per technique.

Research note: several primary PDFs (Game AI Pro chapters, GDC papers, Guerrilla slides) were read in full from their text layers. The shared web-search budget ran out before the last few queries (MGSV radio check-ins, recent 2024–2026 talks, cooperative pathfinding). Those items are listed under Gaps.

## 1. Which architectures shipped for guard and soldier AI, what did developers report, and how is utility AI used and combined with state machines?

### Takeaway
Shipped games converge on layers. A small, prioritized top level picks the mode: an FSM of "skills" in The Last of Us, prioritized behaviour-tree lists in Halo 2, planner goals in F.E.A.R. Inside that mode, continuous numeric scoring picks the specific position or action: The Last of Us's post selectors, Killzone's position-evaluation functions, Guild Wars 2's utility considerations. A separate coordinator layer sits above the individuals. The Last of Us (an FSM of skills, multiplicative post-selector scores within each skill, and a Combat Coordinator that hands out roles) is the closest published template for "awareness states with utility scoring inside each state". The planners (F.E.A.R.'s GOAP, Guerrilla's HTN) shipped and worked. Their most transferable lessons, though, are architectural: keep goals separate from actions, keep squad logic out of individual logic, avoid dithering, and say decisions out loud. None of those requires a planner.

### Cited Findings

#### F.E.A.R. (Monolith, 2005): GOAP planner over a three-state FSM [shipped: F.E.A.R., 2005]
- The character FSM has only three states (Goto, Animate, UseSmartObject). A* plans sequences of actions as well as paths. The planner resembles STRIPS but adds a cost per action, so A* finds the cheapest plan that satisfies a goal — [Orkin, GDC 2006 paper (course copy)](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf); [GDC Vault entry](https://gdcvault.com/play/1013282/Three-States-and-a-Plan)
- The motivation was that the team had a single AI programmer. In No One Lives Forever 2, each goal embedded its own FSM, so adding a late behaviour (turning on lights when entering a dark room) meant editing every goal's machine. With GOAP that is one new action with an effect, plus a precondition on Goto — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- Behaviour comes from layering goals and actions, never from authored transitions. KillEnemy is satisfied by Attack, AttackMelee or AttackFromCover; Dodge by DodgeShuffle or DodgeRoll; Cover by GotoNode; Ambush handles invalidated cover. The planner finds the dependencies at run time — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- Re-planning uses failures recorded in shared working memory. A soldier finds a door held shut, tries to kick it, then dives through a window — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- The combat dialogue system was separate from the planner and hand-hooked into code in many places. Orkin names that cost and proposes treating speech as planner actions in future — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)

#### Halo 2 (Bungie, 2004): behaviour DAG with binary relevancy [shipped: Halo 2, 2004]
- The behaviour graph is a DAG, so one behaviour can sit in several places. The core has on the order of 50 behaviours, about 115 overall. Prioritized-list selection is the most common scheme: a higher-priority sibling can interrupt the running one on a later tick. Other schemes are sequential, looping, probabilistic and one-off — [Isla, GDC 2005 proceedings](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- Relevancy is deliberately a binary test, not a float: with twenty or more options, tuning floating-point scores became practically impossible — [Isla 2005](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- Other mechanisms (same source):
  - **Impulses:** lightweight triggers that run a behaviour in place or jump to its native place in the tree.
  - **Stimulus behaviours:** inserted by event handlers (a leader dying, for example) and kept for one or two seconds.
  - **Custom behaviours:** added per character type in a preprocess.
  - **Tags:** bitvectors for vehicle state and alertness that unlock or lock whole branches cheaply.
- Only running behaviours keep state (about 4 layers): the article estimates about 25K against 192K for allocating state to every behaviour. Persistent per-behaviour data such as grenade cooldowns lives elsewhere — [Isla 2005](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- Reported failure modes:
  - **Dithering:** rapid switching between actions.
  - **Opacity:** the AI looks random rather than intentional, and the team loses control as complexity grows.
  - **Styles:** misconfigured ones block every behaviour.
  - **Flat knowledge model:** a list of potential targets with no spatial relations.
  - **Hacks:** impulse code that bypasses the normal path.

  The AI also has to run at 30 Hz or faster. Character files inherit parameter blocks; without inheritance about 10,350 numbers would need maintaining (3 parameters × ~115 behaviours × ~30 character types) — [Isla 2005](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)

#### Killzone (Guerrilla, 2004): position evaluation functions [shipped: Killzone, 2004]
- Tactics are procedures that score candidate waypoints with a weighted sum of factors. In the worked attack-position example: proximity weighs 20; line of fire to the primary threat weighs 40 with partial cover, 20 without; cover from secondary threats 20; inside preferred range 10. Personality and squad constraints change the weights and filters — [Beij & Straatman, GDC Europe 2005 slides](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/gdce05_killzone_ai.pdf)
- Reported pros: behaviour reused across games, quick level prototyping, fights anywhere, and it combines with scripting. Cons: harder to control in detail, harder to test. Up to 14 AI fought at once — [Beij & Straatman 2005](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/gdce05_killzone_ai.pdf)

#### Killzone 2 multiplayer bots (2009) and Horizon Zero Dawn (2017): HTN planning [shipped]
- Killzone 2's bots were layered: a commander per faction, squads, then individual bots. Individual and squad behaviour ran on an HTN planner. Slides list a domain of 360 methods, 1,048 branches and 138 behaviours — [Guerrilla summary page](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/killzone-2-multiplayer-bots); [Verweij thesis page](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/a-hierarchically-layered-multiplayer-bot-system-for-a-first-person-shooter); domain sizes from [slides on SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/guerrillagames/killzone-2-multiplayer-bots) **[low confidence: numbers came via a search summary, not the extracted slide text]**
- Horizon Zero Dawn combined HTN planning with utility-based decisions. It also built navigation for agents of varying size and systems to coordinate groups — [Guerrilla, The AI of Horizon Zero Dawn, 2017](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/the-ai-of-horizon-zero-dawn). The slides' notes explain the HTN: methods are made of branches, tried top to bottom. Each branch has declarative preconditions (nested and/or/not) solved by a backtracking matcher over world-state facts. Abstract tasks decompose recursively into concrete ones, and the planner backtracks when nothing applies — [HZD slides](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/The-AI-of-Horizon-Zero-Dawn.pdf)

#### The Last of Us (Naughty Dog, 2013): FSM of skills, behaviour stack, post selectors [shipped: The Last of Us, 2013]
- **Skills and behaviours.** Top-level states ("skills": panic, advance, melee, gun combat, hide, investigate, scripted, flank) form a prioritized FSM. Every frame, each skill is asked whether it wants to run, and the highest priority wins. Each skill has its own internal state machine (gun combat can be advancing or backing away). Skills push simple behaviours (MoveToLocation, StandAndShoot, TakeCover) onto a behaviour stack — [McIntosh, Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Post selectors (utility within skills).** Candidate positions ("posts") are scored by post selectors: 17 shipped, written in a LISP-like script that hot-reloads. Each criterion returns a normalized 0–1 value. The criteria are multiplied, and the highest-rated post wins. A selector is a list of criteria such as path valid, available, not behind the player, and a distance curve (0 at 3 m, 1 at 5 m) — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Continuous evaluation.** Every selector is evaluated for every post all the time, so a skill change has no delay. Gathering the data (raycasts, paths) runs in a separate system on earlier frames: the closest 20 covers per NPC, up to 160 rays per frame, 4 rays per cover, and paths refreshed round-robin about every 0.5 s — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Failure found in focus tests.** NPCs rushed toward the player to reach the "best" cover. The fix was a criterion that rejects covers whose path runs toward the player for long. Half of the implementation decisions came from watching players' videos, and spatial analysis was the biggest single win — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)

#### Splinter Cell: Blacklist (Ubisoft Toronto, 2013): awareness models and a group-behaviour layer [shipped: Splinter Cell Blacklist, 2013]
- Awareness is defined as discrete mental states reached progressively through perception. In practice it is a detection bar with an intermediate "saw something, go investigate" state. Four criteria for success: fairness, consistency (similar distances and timings every time, varied barks and animations), good feedback, and plausibility — [Walsh, Game AI Pro 2 ch. 28](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf)
- A group-behaviour system takes control of the NPCs in a group (a conversation, for example) and handles their events first. It is also how the AI blocks exploits such as the player sniping from an unreachable rooftop — [Walsh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf)

#### Left 4 Dead (Valve, 2008): actions with explicit, explained transitions, plus the AI Director [shipped: Left 4 Dead, 2008]
- Each actor has Locomotion, Body, Vision and Intention components. Intention holds concurrent Behaviours (described as an HFSM plus a stack) made of Actions. Actions have OnStart, Update, OnEnd, OnSuspend and OnResume, and return transitions: Continue, ChangeTo, SuspendFor or Done. Every transition carries a reason string ("the player I was chasing died") for live debug output — [Booth, The AI Systems of Left 4 Dead, 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Transitions are atomic, and only Action A can transition itself to B. Events propagate to the innermost child action, then to buried actions, then to the parent. Contextual queries (which threat is more dangerous, should I pick this up?) propagate the same way and let concurrent behaviours coordinate — [Booth 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)

#### Alien: Isolation (Creative Assembly, 2014): omniscient director plus a non-cheating alien [shipped; secondary]
- **Two tiers.** A director that always knows where the player is periodically hints that location to the alien. The alien itself must find the player through its own sensors. The director assigns jobs through a utility job system, where each job has a task, a location and a priority; the priority decides whether the alien finishes, blends or interrupts its current action — [Thompson, Game Developer, relaying Andy Bray's 2016 nucl.ai talk](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation) **[secondary]**
- **The tree.** The behaviour tree has over 100 nodes, about 30 of them at the top level. Some behaviours start locked and unlock as conditions are met. Unlocks are never triggered by player deaths, so the player never faces an advantage he cannot diagnose. Campaign-point triggers unlock anything still locked, so the alien keeps pace with an improving player. Only two teleports exist in the whole campaign, both for cutscenes — [Thompson](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation) **[secondary]**

#### DOOM (id Software, 2016): hierarchical FSMs and attack tokens [shipped; secondary]
- The AI uses hierarchical FSMs, carried over from id Tech 5 and Rage — [Thompson, Cyber Demons](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-) **[secondary]**. Tokens are covered under question 3.

#### Hitman (IO Interactive, 2016): behaviour trees [shipped; secondary]
- Hitman's core AI relies on behaviour trees. The architecture was built for Absolution (2012) and extended in 2016 and 2018 — [The AI of Hitman (2016), Game Developer community blog](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-ai-of-hitman-2016-) **[secondary]**

#### Metal Gear Solid V (Kojima Productions, 2015): four alert states [shipped; low confidence]
- A guide describes four main behaviour states: calm; curiosity (a suspicious sight or sound the guard may walk over to check); increased vigilance (after an unsuppressed shot or a found body; the guard tells his colleagues and they form a group to hunt the player); and full alert (a guard who detected the player and was not stopped quickly raises the alarm) — [GamePressure MGSV guide](https://www.gamepressure.com/mgs5thephantompain/enemies-states-of-alert/z27b18) **[low confidence: guide; no developer source]**

#### Thief: The Dark Project (Looking Glass, 1998): awareness as discrete states with asymmetric filters [shipped: Thief, 1998]
- Awareness is a set of discrete states for how sure an AI is of an object's presence, location and identity, and higher-level alertness states map onto them. Alertness feeds back into the senses; for example, it changes which view cones are relevant — [Leonard, GDC 2003 article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/building-an-ai-sensory-system-examining-the-design-of-i-thief-the-dark-project-i-)
- Rises pass through a time filter whose delay belongs to the current state, not the target state. That delay is how reaction time and "forgiveness" are implemented; past the threshold, awareness jumps straight to the goal state. Falls go through a "capacitor" that steps down through every intermediate state. When pulses stop, the AI gets some "free knowledge" scaled by its state, which looks like deduction — [Leonard 2003](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/building-an-ai-sensory-system-examining-the-design-of-i-thief-the-dark-project-i-)
- Design lessons: keep the grey zone between safe and detected wide; use few player-facing inputs and discrete outputs so designers and players can predict outcomes; avoid "peek-a-boo" (the player is seen before seeing the guard). The example AI has five view cones, including a backward motion-only cone. Sensing took a large share of the AI budget, so update detail and frequency were scaled — [Leonard 2003](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/building-an-ai-sensory-system-examining-the-design-of-i-thief-the-dark-project-i-)

#### Utility AI and how it combines with state machines
- **Infinite Axis Utility System, Guild Wars 2: Heart of Thorns** [shipped: GW2 HoT, 2015]:
  - Each possible action is a Decision Score Evaluator (DSE). A DSE's considerations take raw inputs normalized to [0, 1], pass them through response curves, and multiply them.
  - Any consideration scoring 0 disqualifies the decision and stops evaluation early, so cheap considerations go first. The best-scoring DSE wins each think cycle.
  - Runtime and cooldown considerations break repetitive cycles and stop two decisions strobing.
  - Most curves come from a small palette of presets; arbitrary curves are kept behind an "advanced mode" because they intimidate designers.
  - Mandatory "switch" considerations (don't interrupt a running action, don't move while immobilized) are kept as data rather than hard-coded so designers can bypass them.

  [Lewis, Game AI Pro 3 ch. 13](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- **Dual-utility reasoning, Zoo Tycoon 2** [shipped: Zoo Tycoon 2, 2004]: each option has a rank and a weight. Options with weight ≤ 0 are dropped, then everything below the highest rank, then (optionally) anything below a percentage of the best weight. The rest are picked by weight-based random. Animals' need-driven options were rank 0; context-locked sets (a koala up a tree) were around rank 5, so the koala did not pop out of the tree. Pure relative utility can choose silly low-weight options; pure absolute utility is rigidly predictable — [Dill, Game AI Pro 2 ch. 3](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter03_Dual-Utility_Reasoning.pdf)
- **The Sims** [shipped: The Sims, 2000; secondary]: motives (hunger, hygiene, fun, energy, bladder, social, comfort, room) drive the choice. Objects advertise how much they satisfy each motive, and a Sim picks at random among the top-scoring interactions so it does not look robotic — [draft wiki article on AI in The Sims](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/User:ElijahPepe/Artificial_intelligence_in_The_Sims); [GMTK transcript](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/gmtk-the-genius-ai-behind-the-sims) **[secondary, low confidence]**
- **Utility inside a state machine, in shipped games:**
  - The Last of Us: a skills FSM chooses the mode; multiplied criterion scores choose the post — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
  - Killzone: action type and personality select the evaluation function; a weighted sum picks the waypoint — [Beij & Straatman](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/gdce05_killzone_ai.pdf)
  - Zoo Tycoon 2: rank acts as the state — [Dill](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter03_Dual-Utility_Reasoning.pdf)
  - Alien: Isolation: the director's utility jobs feed a behaviour tree — [Thompson](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation) **[secondary]**

### Inferences
- **Recommended shape for Lab E**, a synthesis of The Last of Us, Thief and Guild Wars 2 (not a direct quote of any one):
  - Awareness states (Unaware, Curious, Suspicious, Searching, Combat) as a prioritized FSM of skills, each with its own small internal machine.
  - Inside each state, IAUS-style DSEs pick the action and post.
  - Thief-style asymmetric filters on transitions: rises delayed by the current state's dwell time; falls stepping down one state at a time to a floor, which is the raised baseline.
  - Halo-style stimulus behaviours for the 1–2 s reactions.
  - L4D-style reason strings on every transition, feeding the debug overlay and the debrief's chain of causes (rule 8).
- **Fit notes, one per technique:**
  - **GOAP (F.E.A.R.).** Deterministic if the action list order and A* tie-breaks are fixed and the search is capped by node count. The plan has to live in `sim.state` as plain data (action ids plus an index). Probably overkill for about 10 guard actions: it costs explainability, and the decoupling lesson can be had without the planner.
  - **Prioritized behaviour tree / DAG (Halo 2).** Very good fit: binary relevancy is trivially deterministic. "Only running nodes keep state" maps to a small plain-data stack of node ids per guard. Stimulus behaviours map to percept events with an expiry tick.
  - **HTN (Killzone 2, Horizon Zero Dawn).** Deterministic if fact matching iterates in a stable order and backtracking is capped. Better suited to the camp commander's multi-step plans (seal the camp, then sweep, then evacuate the target) than to per-guard reactions.
  - **Skills FSM plus post selectors plus coordinator (The Last of Us).** Best direct fit. TLOU's millisecond budgets must become iteration budgets (rule 18): round-robin raycasts and paths with the cursor kept in state. Multiplied 0–1 scores are exact in IEEE doubles if the multiplication order is fixed.
  - **IAUS (Guild Wars 2).** Fits "utility within states". Curves live in `tuning.ts` (rules 4 and 16). Build curves from `+ - * /` polynomials, as in the chapter's y = 1 − (x − 1)^4, or `detmath`'s `dpow`; never `Math.pow`. Break score ties by guard id or option index.
  - **Dual utility (Zoo Tycoon 2).** Fits well. Rank bands can be the awareness states. Weight-based random draws come from the guard's own stream (`ctx.draw(id, 'decide', …)`), so removing a guard never shifts another guard's choices (rule 3).
  - **Advertisements (The Sims).** Fits workers' and the target's routines: an object's advertisement is static world data, which the mind already holds. Not for threat responses.
  - **Actions with reason strings (L4D).** Nearly free and directly serves rule 8. Store the reason as a short code in state; the debrief expands it.
  - **Omniscient directors (L4D, Alien: Isolation).** Their information flow conflicts with rule 13; Sightline should take the pacing ideas, not the knowledge (see question 3).

## 2. Beliefs and search: last known position, occupancy maps, search behaviour, how searches end, how suspicion decays

### Takeaway
Two shipped ideas carry most of the weight. First, keep a perception-filtered belief record per target: position plus timestamp (The Last of Us), Halo 2's "props", Thief's "sense links". Second, model uncertainty as a grid probability that spreads outward from the last known position over time and is cleared wherever a searcher can currently see; The Last of Us shipped this as its search map, Isla built Third Eye Crime around it as occupancy maps. Searches end through explicit cycle rules (The Last of Us sends a single NPC to check after 10 s unseen) and through timeouts and dialogue. The published failure is that searches run far too long; The Last of Us found about 2 minutes on average and had to cut it to about 30 s.

### Cited Findings

#### Belief records
- **The Last of Us** [shipped, 2013]: on perceiving the player, an NPC creates an entity record with a location and a timestamp and signals the new location to every other NPC. If no NPC perceives the player, his believed location is never updated. Mostly no cheating — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Halo 2 "props"** [shipped, 2004]: actor-side records of objects, gated by the actor's perception filter, so beliefs can differ from the world. They also hold per-object, per-behaviour data such as when the actor last meleed it, search failures and pathfinding failures. Search records failure against a specific target, so the actor stops pursuing that target without disabling search altogether — [Isla 2005](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- **Thief "sense links"** [shipped, 1998]: a link holds awareness toward an entity or a position, plus time, location, line of sight and cached values. They are the AI's primary memory — [Leonard 2003](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/building-an-ai-sensory-system-examining-the-design-of-i-thief-the-dark-project-i-)
- **Yuka (open source, MIT, JavaScript):** a `MemorySystem` holds `MemoryRecord`s (entity, timeBecameVisible, timeLastSensed, lastSensedPosition, visible) and forgets records older than `memorySpan` (default 1 s) — [Yuka source](https://github.com/Mugen87/yuka) (checked via the GitHub API)

#### Search maps and occupancy maps
- **The Last of Us search map** [shipped, 2013]: once the NPCs are aware of the player, search procedurally partitions the map and sends NPCs to cover it. The search map is a grid. While the player is visible, only his cell is active. Once he is lost, active cells bleed into their neighbours over time, and any cell currently visible to an NPC is cleared every frame, using the exposure map (a bitmap of what NPCs can see) — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Occupancy maps (Isla, Third Eye Crime)** [shipped: Third Eye Crime, Moonshot Games, 2013/2014]: Isla's AIIDE 2013 paper describes a commercial stealth puzzler whose core mechanic is occupancy maps, a rare case of an AI algorithm being the game's core mechanic — [AIIDE 2013 page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12663); [Semantic Scholar record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1609/aiide.v9i1.12663?fields=title,abstract,openAccessPdf,year,authors). The companion GDC 2013 talk (Isla and Christian Baekkelund) is about building a whole game around one AI technique — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1018057/From-the-Behavior-Up-When). In the game, the protagonist can telepathically predict where enemies will move — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/301420). Reviews describe the guards' search patterns painted onto the map in red — [AppSpy review](https://www.appspy.com/review/8453/third-eye-crime) **[low confidence: attribution among the reviews in a search summary is uncertain]**
- **Generic description:** a grid of connected cells, each holding the probability that the target is there. Each update a diffusion step moves part of each cell's probability to its neighbours, and an NPC moves where it best expects to find the target. A forum commenter notes that this is essentially a hidden Markov model rediscovered by games — [GameDev.net thread](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/594674-ai-searching-for-the-player-intelligently/?page=1) **[low confidence: forum and search-summary level; the primary text was blocked by bot protection]**
- **Alternative representation:** particle filters ("simulacra") estimate specific candidate positions rather than probabilities over regions — [Darken & Anderegg, AI Game Programming Wisdom 4 (abstract)](https://faculty.nps.edu/cjdarken/pubs/simulacra-abstract.html) **[secondary]**

#### Recent research on guard search
- **Xu & Verbrugge, AIIDE 2025: composite potential fields** [research-only, 2025]. A guard scores nodes with a weighted sum of three fields:
  - **Information:** a "scent" of the player, reset on detection, spread by breadth-first search with falloff, and decaying over time.
  - **Confidence:** guard coverage, which discourages revisits.
  - **Connectivity:** a static field favouring dead ends and corridors.

  On detection the weights shift toward information, then decay back to their base values. Results on a grid with a MaxMin player, the player at 120% speed and the guards' view at 80%: capture rate 0.80 (mean 31.7 s) against 0.56 for a staleness FSM, 0.12 for a plain FSM, 0.04 for a random walk and 0.95 for a cheating chaser. Decisions run every 1 s, the method is training-free, and tuning was manual. It covers one guard (little multi-guard coordination) and was not tested with human players — [arXiv 2508.18527](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18527v1); [AIIDE 2025 page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/36819)
- **Al Enezi & Verbrugge (McGill)** [research-only, 2020–2024]: a "staleness" metric that grows with time since a region was last visited drives where guards search ("Dynamic Guard Patrol in Stealth Games", AIIDE 2020). "Skeleton-based multi-agent opponent search" (IEEE CoG 2021) coordinates several guards over a skeleton of the environment. Al Enezi's 2024 thesis covers guard search and dialogue — [DBLP CoG 2021 record](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/cig/EneziV21); [thesis](https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/downloads/v692tc945) **[methods not read in full]**

#### Where to look while searching
- **Alien: Isolation** [secondary]: areas of interest are hand-placed or generated (for example, from player noise). The alien visits a perimeter of nearby points in a deliberately sub-optimal order, prioritized by visibility, and the backtracking reads as doubt. There are two kinds of point: search points (it moves there) and spot points (it stays and turns to look). Designers can mark areas it never looks at, to prevent unfair detections — [Thompson](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)
- **F.E.A.R. squad Search** [shipped]: the squad splits into pairs that cover each other and search rooms in an area systematically — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- **Splinter Cell (TEAS)** [shipped: Conviction 2010, Blacklist 2013]: the world is split into areas connected by choke nodes such as doors and windows, each with fallback position nodes. Area metadata includes size, type, and how many NPCs should enter the area for search or combat. NPCs who cannot see the player cover the chokes into his area instead of rushing in or staring at walls; Conviction had 12 active NPCs, more than the available covers. Blacklist auto-generated chokes; auto-generating the areas was future work — [Walsh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf)
- **The Last of Us "open posts"**: points around the player's last known position, filtered by raycasts toward it. NPCs sent to check that position go to one of them — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **The Last of Us Part II** [shipped, 2020; press]: co-director Anthony Newman described a new awareness state between knowing and not knowing the player's position, in which enemies search in the general direction — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/the-last-of-us-2s-upgraded-enemy-ai-will-make-for-/1100-6470119/). Enemies call out the last known position, and killing one alerts the squad — [Military Times, 2025](https://www.militarytimes.com/off-duty/military-culture/2025/03/18/the-last-of-us-part-ii-nails-combat-better-than-most-war-games/) **[secondary, press]**

#### How searches end, and how suspicion decays
- **The Last of Us combat-to-search cycle:** NPCs surround and advance. If they have closed in as far as they can and have not seen the player for 10 s or more, one NPC is chosen to approach and check, and if the player is gone they move to search. In focus tests this cycle averaged nearly 2 minutes and felt unresponsive. The fix was a cheat: if the player moved more than 5 m from where the NPCs believed he was, an NPC approached immediately. That brought the cycle to about 30 s — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **The Last of Us perception timer:** it counts up each frame the player is seen and down each frame he is not. He counts as perceived at about 1–2 s for a typical NPC; the threshold is much lower in combat and much higher if the NPC has never perceived him — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Thief:** awareness falls gradually through every intermediate state (the "capacitor") — [Leonard 2003](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/building-an-ai-sensory-system-examining-the-design-of-i-thief-the-dark-project-i-)
- **Splinter Cell: Blacklist:** transitions are spoken. Example: after a grenade on a rooftop, one guard asks if the player is still up there, another doubts it and proposes spreading out, and the NPCs move to search. An investigation of a minor event that finds nothing returns the NPCs to their conversation with a rejoin line — [Walsh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf)
- **Metal Gear Solid series:** Alert (reinforcements, attack), then Evasion (search the vicinity after losing the player), then Caution (more vigilant patrols) — [Metal Gear Wiki, Enemy status](https://metalgear.fandom.com/wiki/Enemy_status) **[low confidence: fan wiki, via search summary]**. In MGSV, guards eventually calm down and return to routine; the guide gives no timer — [GamePressure MGSV guide](https://www.gamepressure.com/mgs5thephantompain/enemies-states-of-alert/z27b18) **[low confidence: guide]**
- **Sniper Elite 3** [shipped, 2014]: a ghost marks the last known position, enemies search around it alone or in groups, and they eventually stop if the player gets far away into cover — [Engadget review](https://www.engadget.com/2014-07-11-sniper-elite-3-review-war-and-pieces.html); [GameWatcher preview](https://www.gamewatcher.com/previews/sniper-elite-3-preview/10466) **[secondary]**
- **Ghost Recon Wildlands** [shipped, 2017]: break line of sight and hide until the Unidad patrol timer expires — [GamePressure tips](https://www.gamepressure.com/ghost-recon-wildlands/general-tips/z49803) **[low confidence: guide]**
- **Left 4 Dead intensity:** decays toward zero over time but never while infected are actively engaging the survivor (a template for decaying camp tension) — [Booth 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)

#### Beliefs about dead or missing allies
- **Blacklist "disappearing NPC":** if NPC A has been within earshot of NPC B for 10 s, and then B stays silent for 5 more seconds (because he is dead), an investigation event is generated with a long cooldown against repeats and exploits. Modelling "I stopped hearing him" directly was judged too much work for the gain — [Walsh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf)
- **Blacklist context:** if an NPC dies silently during a group investigation, the group behaviour waits, then sends the survivor to investigate where he died, calling out to his comrade. A body seen during a search causes agitation and a call for help; the same body in a war zone is ignored — [Walsh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf)
- **MGSV:** leaving a body where it can be found, or firing unsuppressed, raises guards to "increased vigilance", and they form a group to hunt the player — [GamePressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/mgs5thephantompain/enemies-states-of-alert/z27b18) **[low confidence: guide]**

#### Where the shots seemed to come from
- **Killzone line-of-fire table:** tactical decisions need hundreds of line-of-fire checks, so a small precomputed table replaced raycasts: 64 KB for 4,000 waypoints. For every waypoint and radial sector it stores the largest distance from which an attacker in that sector can fire at the waypoint. It covers multiple stances and small movements, is pessimistic about cover, and is "inaccurate but consistent" — [Beij & Straatman 2005](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/gdce05_killzone_ai.pdf)
- **Killzone suppression fire:** reason from the threat's side. Evaluate the attack positions the hidden threat could use, from his perspective, keep those scoring at least 40, and fire at those we can hit — [Beij & Straatman 2005](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/gdce05_killzone_ai.pdf)
- **The Last of Us "combat vector":** the direction of combat from the player's side, computed by averaging NPC positions weighted by recent shots fired. Flanking costs use a fixed shape oriented along it. Exposure-map flanking was unstable from frame to frame — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Sniper Elite 3:** each shot from the same spot escalates. The first makes nearby enemies cautious, the second has them zero in, the third reveals the exact position — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-07-11-sniper-elite-3-review-war-and-pieces.html) / [Giant Bomb](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-41928/) **[low confidence: review-level; attribution between the two reviews unclear]**. Sniper Elite 4 (2017) removed the relocation meter, so noise reveals the position at once and environmental sound masking is the main defence. Players reported the masking as inconsistent — [Game Informer review](https://www.gameinformer.com/games/sniper_elite_4/b/playstation4/archive/2017/02/13/sniper-elite-4-game-informer-review.aspx) **[secondary]**

### Inferences
- **Per-guard last-known-position record** (Halo 2 props, The Last of Us record, Yuka): store `{targetId, x, y, z, tick, confidence, sourceGuard, viaRadio}` in plain data inside each guard's mind. Fits rules 9 and 13 directly. Don't reuse Yuka's records as they are: `-Infinity` serializes to `null` in JSON and its Maps and Vector3s are not plain data; use tick numbers with a sentinel such as −1.
- **Search map / occupancy grid** (The Last of Us, Isla): fits the 2.5 m grid well. A dense valley-wide map per guard is costly to hash and save (for example, 400 m square is 160 × 160 = 25,600 cells; 16 guards would carry about 410,000 numbers). Options:
  - One camp-level belief map owned by the commander, fed only by guard and radio reports (so it stays behind the perception boundary).
  - A bounded window, for example 48 × 48 cells, centred on the incident.
  - Sparse lists of active cells.

  Diffusion and clearing are `+ - * /` only, so the map is deterministic if neighbour order is fixed. Renormalize with an exact sum in a fixed order.
- **Clearing cells by what guards can see** (The Last of Us exposure map): needs per-guard visibility over cells. Precompute cell-to-cell sight on the 2.5 m grid at generation, as a static world table (Killzone-style compression), or raycast a budgeted number of cells per tick with a cursor in state.
- **Potential fields** (Xu & Verbrugge 2025): a cheap research add-on. A confidence field built from guard coverage works as a "don't re-search where a mate just looked" term inside the IAUS search DSE. Unproven with humans.
- **Ending a search:** The Last of Us's 5 m cheat reads true player distance, which rule 13 forbids. The equivalent within the boundary is to trigger the check approach, and then the search, on time since the last percept and on the search map's spread (its entropy or the extent of its active cells), not on where the player really is. Keep a hard time budget per search, as the design doc already does.
- **Suspicion decay:** model it as the L4D intensity rule (no decay while an active stimulus is present) combined with Thief's step-down (one state at a time). Sightline's "settles one level above where it started" is then a floor on the capacitor. Both are rates times `dt` (rule 17).
- **Missing-ally belief:** Blacklist's 10 s plus 5 s earshot rule is cheap and fair. Use perceived voice and footsteps as the "hearing B" input, so it stays percept-based, and hold its cooldown in state.
- **Shot-origin belief** (Killzone table plus The Last of Us combat vector): see question 3's inferences for the counter-sniper sweep. Each guard who heard the crack or report contributes a bearing with error drawn from his own stream. The camp fuses the bearings in a fixed order by guard id.

## 3. Group coordination: commander and squad layers, tokens and tickets, alarms, reinforcements and counter-sniper sweeps

### Takeaway
The proven commander pattern is declarative. List the tasks that need doing, each with a priority, an activation condition and a capacity; then let a cheap greedy assignment place squads or guards to minimize a cost, re-run as tasks switch on and off. Halo 3's Objectives system is the clearest published version, and Killzone 2's bot commander is the same idea with objective weights. Individuals stay autonomous: an order is a goal they weigh (F.E.A.R.). Caps on convergence come from roles or tokens that must be requested and released (The Last of Us's Combat Coordinator, DOOM's tokens), from capacities (Halo 3, Blacklist's area metadata), and from first-witness claiming of events (Blacklist). Published alarm systems (Far Cry, MGSV, Splinter Cell) reward physical play: alarms have locations, can be disabled, and escalation brings more and better-equipped troops. Their documentation is mostly guides.

### Cited Findings

#### Commander and squad layers
- **F.E.A.R.** [shipped, 2005]: a global coordinator periodically re-clusters AI into squads by proximity, and each squad runs zero or one squad behaviour at a time. Simple behaviours are Get-to-Cover, Advance-Cover, Orderly-Advance (single file, the last man facing backward) and Search (in pairs). A squad behaviour fills its slots, sends orders, and monitors progress every tick. AI have goals for following orders, which they weigh against other goals; fleeing danger can trump an order to advance. Complex tactics such as flanking mostly emerged from simple squad behaviours plus individual decisions — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- **Halo 3 Objectives** [shipped: Halo 3, 2007]:
  - **Why it replaced Halo 2's method.** Halo 2 had designers build FSMs of encounter transitions ("imperative"), whose explicit transitions grow as n². Halo 3 ("declarative") lists the tasks that need doing and lets the system decide who does them.
  - **Tasks** form a tree; each has a priority, script fragments for activation, and a capacity. Squads are poured in at the top and filter down (a "plinko machine"). When a higher-priority task activates it pulls squads up; when the task they are in deactivates they are pushed down.
  - **Assignment** respects capacities (a bin-packing problem, NP-hard) and minimizes a cost H(s,t) built from travel distance, coordination, tree balance and nearness to the player. It is greedy: repeatedly assign the cheapest (squad, task) pair that fits. That is O(n²m), acceptable because n and m are small; results are cached and the trees timesliced.
  - **Filters** restrict tasks to certain kinds of guy (character type, vehicle, snipers), implemented as infinite cost. Other options: latch on or off, exhaustion by death count or living count, and one-time assignment.
  - **Case studies:** leader and follower filters, with a "broken" state when the leader dies (no redistribution in or out); a player-pickup task.
  - **Downsides reported:** designers need training; the relationship with scripting was awkward; the squad was not always the right unit to assign (a guy who picks up a sniper rifle should be able to take a sniper task); and the system grew complicated near ship. A wrong H makes the AI look stupid.

  [Isla, GDC 2008 slides (course copy)](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)
- **Killzone 2 bots** [shipped, 2009]:
  - **Objectives** carry weights: Defend Marker near home base 1.0, Advance Waypoint to enemy base 1.0, Attack or Escort Entity on the carrier 2.0.
  - **Squads** are created to fill objectives with a desired, minimum and maximum size, kept balanced, and assigned by proximity to the target. After an objective they persist and the bots revert to autonomous behaviour. Every controlled area gets a Defend objective. Capture targets are chosen from current assignments, nearby friendlies and own spawn points; leftover troops attack less desirable areas ("lone scout").
  - **Area costs** come from an influence map: safe areas almost free, risky ones exponentially worse.
  - **Self-critique:** the squad layer made almost no decisions.

  [Straatman & Champandard, Paris Game AI Conference 2009 slides](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GAIC09_Killzone2Bots_StraatmanChampandard.pdf)

#### Tokens, roles and caps that limit how many engage or investigate
- **The Last of Us Combat Coordinator** [shipped, 2013]:
  - A global object manages roles: Flanker, Approacher, Investigator, StayUpAndAimer and OpportunisticShooter. An NPC calls RequestRole; if it succeeds, it calls AcknowledgeRole, and no one else can take that role until it is released.
  - Some roles go only to the ideal NPC, recomputed each frame (Flanker, by flank-route cost); others are first come, first served (OpportunisticShooter).
  - Only one NPC needs to be shooting at any time, so the others take cover and flank. The OpportunisticShooter drops whatever it is doing, even mid-animation; slow transitions had let rushing players go almost untouched.
  - Distraction sounds give out a single Investigator role. The investigator walks to the best post, plays an animation, and returns to his script if he finds nothing.

  [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Blacklist event claiming:** when an object changes state (a door, a window, a light), an event with a lifetime is created, and the first NPC to witness it claims it and may investigate. Reviewers singled out NPCs noticing opened doors and lights switched off; the developers call it the system with the most return for its cost — [Walsh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf)
- **Blacklist area caps:** TEAS area metadata includes how many NPCs should enter the area for search or combat, and some group behaviours are flagged not to repeat — [Walsh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf)
- **DOOM (2016) tokens** [shipped; secondary]: each attack type has a limited pool of tokens, sized per difficulty. A demon requests a token before attacking and releases it afterward, and demons can take tokens from each other when better placed, so the ones in front of the player never stand idle — [Thompson, Cyber Demons](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-) **[secondary]**. The token idea is credited to id's GDC 2018 talk by Kurt Loudy and Jake Campbell — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024940/Embracing-Push-Forward-Combat-in); [open-source Unreal reimplementation](https://github.com/Lim-Young/UnrealAITokenSystem) **[license not checked]**
- **Guild Wars 2 "already enough allies" consideration:** a charge attack's DSE reads the influence map and scores low when enough allies are already around the target, so a player fighting two enemies doesn't get a third piling on. The author calls it a design constraint easily expressed in utility AI — [Lewis](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)

#### Pacing layers
- **Left 4 Dead AI Director** [shipped, 2008]:
  - **Intensity rules.** Each survivor's intensity rises when injured (in proportion to damage), when incapacitated, when pulled off a ledge, and when a nearby infected dies (inversely with distance). It decays toward zero, but not while infected are engaging. The Director tracks the maximum over the survivors.
  - **Cycle.** Build Up (full threat population until intensity crosses the peak), then Sustain Peak for 3–5 s, then Peak Fade (minimal population until intensity falls out of the peak range; relax does not start until a natural break), then Relax for 30–45 s or until the survivors travel far enough, then Build Up again.
  - **Motivation.** Inspired by Counter-Strike's spiky pacing: constant combat is fatiguing and long inactivity boring.

  [Booth 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- **L4D "structured unpredictability":**
  - Mobs of 20–30 spawn at random intervals of 90–180 s on Normal; 75% come from behind the survivors' "flow distance".
  - Mob size grows from a minimum just after a spawn to a maximum over time.
  - Boss events (Tank, Witch, Nothing) are shuffled along the route with no successive repeats.
  - Wanderer counts in an area are zeroed when any survivor can see the area, and during Relax.
  - Populations exist only in the "active area set" around the survivors.

  [Booth 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- **Alien: Isolation menace gauge** [secondary]: it rises when the alien is within a short walk of the player, when the player can see the alien, and when the alien is close on the motion tracker and could reach him quickly (more slowly if it is in another room). When menace stays peaked, the director sends the alien back to the vents (the "backstage" state), choosing vents near where the player seems to be heading — [Thompson](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)

#### Alarms that must be physically raised, and reinforcements
- **Far Cry 3 and 4 outposts** [shipped: Far Cry 3 2012, Far Cry 4 2014]: a guard who spots the player typically runs to an alarm panel, and the alarm brings reinforcements. Shooting a panel's control box disables that panel. Switching one off by hand disables the outpost's whole alarm system (Far Cry 3). Far Cry 4 separates "no alarm" from "undetected" in its achievements — [Prima Far Cry 3 outposts guide](https://primagames.com/eguides/far-cry-3-with-interactive-map-eguide/essentials/key-locations/outposts); [GamerGuides Far Cry 3](https://gamerguides.com/far-cry-3/guide/introduction/gameplay/outposts); [Tread Lightly achievement](https://trueachievements.com//a194724/tread-lightly-achievement) **[low confidence: guides]**
- **MGSV** [shipped, 2015]: by default only the nearest enemies respond to an alarm, and in the open only a few. Base personnel may contact other bases and outposts, raising alertness more widely; destroying rooftop antennas or cutting power beforehand prevents this. Reflex Mode briefly slows time when a guard detects you; neutralizing him in that window prevents the alarm — [GamePressure MGSV states of alert](https://www.gamepressure.com/mgs5thephantompain/enemies-states-of-alert/z27b18); [GamePressure MGSV combat advice](https://www.gamepressure.com/mgs5thephantompain/general-combat-advices/zc7b1b) **[low confidence: guides]**. At higher response levels soldiers are more numerous and heavily armed, and reinforcements are quicker and larger — [SegmentNext](https://segmentnext.com/mgsv-phantom-pain-enemies-response-system-defense-vehicles-guide/) **[low confidence: guide]**
- **Ghost Recon Wildlands** [shipped, 2017]: when Unidad troops detect and engage you, an escalating patrol level appears (1 upward, shown as chevrons, GTA-like). Higher levels send more and heavier reinforcements (troops, armoured vehicles, rocket helicopters) — [Prima Wildlands Unidad guide](https://primagames.com/eguides/tom-clancys-ghost-recon-wildlands-eguide/the-factions/unidad-forces) **[low confidence: guide; thresholds differ between sources]**
- **Splinter Cell series:** alarms escalate in stages. Pandora Tomorrow guards put on flak jackets and helmets; Chaos Theory has four alarm stages. Double Agent's Iceland briefing lists stage 2 body armour, stage 3 helmets, stage 4 fortified positions — [Splinter Cell Wiki: Alarm](https://splintercell.fandom.com/wiki/Alarm); [Iceland – Sabotage](https://splintercell.fandom.com/wiki/Iceland_%E2%80%93_Sabotage) **[low confidence: fan wiki; the stage-to-gear mapping differs by game]**
- **F.E.A.R. implied reinforcements:** a squad's last survivor calls for reinforcements, but no reinforcement mechanism existed. Players read the next enemies they met as the reinforcements — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)

#### Counter-sniper and unreachable-position responses
- **Blacklist "unreachable above":** when the player is seen on an unreachable rooftop, the group behaviour runs a scripted-but-systemic exchange. One NPC calls for a grenade, another throws it under covering fire. If the player slips away, they discuss and switch to search. If the player keeps killing them, they fall back to protective cover in alternation. Seeing him on the same rooftop after a massacre makes them call it out and run for safety rather than engage. The dialogue is a discussion between guards, not one-way callouts — [Walsh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf)
- **Killzone's line-of-fire table and suppression from the threat's perspective** (question 2) are the published building blocks for "where could he be firing from".

### Inferences
- **Camp commander = Halo 3 Objectives over guards** (not squads, which avoids Halo's "wrong bucket" complaint) with Killzone 2's objective weights. Tasks: patrol routes, posts, search sectors (cells of the search map), "check on post X", "man alarm box", "escort target", "sweep perch P". Each task has a priority, an activation condition read from camp beliefs, and a capacity. Run a greedy assignment with cost = path distance + role suitability + coverage balance, with ties broken by task index then guard id. Store assignments as plain data and re-run on the commander's tick (for example 1 Hz, as a system in its phase). Replacing a missing patrol falls out of this: a patrol task whose holder is believed dead or missing is vacated and refilled by the cheapest eligible guard. 5–16 guards × ~30 tasks is tiny. Fully deterministic.
- **Orders as percepts** (F.E.A.R.): the commander's order reaches a guard's mind as data with a delay (voice or radio), and the guard's utility weighs it ("follow order" is a DSE). That keeps rule 13 intact and lets self-preservation override.
- **Convergence cap = role tokens** (The Last of Us Combat Coordinator, DOOM tokens, Blacklist claiming) held in camp state as `{role, incidentId, holder, sinceTick}`. Requests are processed in a fixed order within the phase (by guard id, or "ideal holder by cost" like The Last of Us's Flanker). Outside combat, cap investigators per incident (The Last of Us uses one) and searchers per area (Blacklist's area metadata), and add a GW2-style "enough allies already there" consideration.
- **Pacing layer:** keep L4D's shape (build, sustain 3–5 s, fade, relax 30–45 s) but drive it from camp beliefs, not the player's true state. An omniscient pacing input leaks knowledge across the perception boundary: a cap that relaxes only when the player is really far away tells the guards something. Its role is to throttle (restricting how many guards converge, delaying escalation steps), never to inform. That makes it a legitimate system outside the brains, as long as it reads only camp state.
- **Variety without unfairness:** L4D-style random intervals (reinforcement truck arrival in [min, max] s, check-in timing jitter) drawn from the truck's or operator's own stream, plus shuffle bags with no immediate repeats (stored in state as an array plus an index).
- **Physical alarm** (Far Cry panels, MGSV antennas, Reflex window): the alarm box is an entity with a position and state (working, disabled, raised). A guard's "raise alarm" DSE needs a path to it. The 4–8 s run is a motor journey the player can interrupt, which matches MGSV's interruptible call and Lab E's exit test. Lockdown is set only by the alarm box's or radio's state change, never by a brain directly.
- **Counter-sniper sweep:** at generation, precompute Killzone-style data for each camp-relevant cell. Per 16 bearing sectors, store the farthest distance from which a prone or standing shooter can see it, and list candidate perches (cells with cover or elevation that see camp cells). At run time, take the camp's fused shot bearing (The Last of Us combat-vector style), intersect it with the sector, rank perches by a utility (fits the bearing, sightlines to the victims, reachable), and post the top N as sweep tasks with capacities. Static tables live in the world, which the mind is allowed to read. Blacklist's "unreachable above" logic is the template for perches the guards cannot reach: suppress, flush, fall back, call the marksman.

## 4. Communication: radio check-ins, delays, unanswered calls

### Takeaway
The "unanswered check-in" mechanic is long established. In Metal Gear Solid 2, headquarters periodically checks on guards, and a guard who doesn't answer (because he was knocked out) brings an investigation team and possibly a Caution phase. Its MGSV form (calls to the command post that can be interrupted) is documented only by players. Developers stress that coordination must be audible, or players never notice it. F.E.A.R. and Blacklist voice decisions as two-guard exchanges, and Blacklist cycles from specific barks to generic ones so memorable lines don't repeat. No shipped game found here publishes explicit radio-delay numbers. The Last of Us broadcasts sightings to every NPC at once, and tuned the pacing around that rather than modelling delay.

### Cited Findings
- **Metal Gear Solid 2** [shipped, 2001]: HQ sometimes checks on guards, even knocked-out ones. If one doesn't respond, an investigation team arrives to check on him, which can trigger Caution mode — [StrategyWiki MGS2 gameplay](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Metal_Gear_Solid_2:_Sons_of_Liberty/Gameplay) **[low confidence: community wiki, via search summary; the page itself failed to load]**
- **MGSV** [shipped, 2015]: radio calls between guards and the command post do take place but are drawn out over minutes. They can be interrupted by tranquilizing, injuring or killing the caller, or throwing a grenade near him. After a call finishes, guards may enter combat again and fire at the last known position — [speedrun.com user comment thread](https://www.speedrun.com/users/AgentA/comments) **[low confidence: one player's testing]**. Guides advise waiting until a suspicious guard has radioed that all is well before taking him out — [Supercheats MGSV stealth ops](https://www.supercheats.com/metal-gear-solid-v-the-phantom-pain/walkthrough/stealth-ops) **[low confidence: guide]**. Base personnel can contact other bases unless the antennas or power are cut — [GamePressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/mgs5thephantompain/enemies-states-of-alert/z27b18) **[low confidence: guide]**
- **The Last of Us** [shipped]: a perceiving NPC signals the player's new location to all other NPCs immediately. NPCs had to make it obvious to the player when they lost him, and to notice when their friends died. Dialogue was layered on after the decision-making to communicate it — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **The Last of Us Part II** [shipped, 2020; press]: enemies call each other by name and talk constantly, and the one who first spots the player passes the information on, rather than everyone knowing at once — [StopGame (Russian)](https://stopgame.ru/newsdata/34951) **[secondary, press]**
- **F.E.A.R.:** the squad layer sees the whole situation and picks the dialogue sequence that shows coordination was intended. Wherever possible, lines are exchanges between two characters rather than one announcement: one guard asks another if he sees anything; a wounded guard is asked how he is and answers. Dialogue also explains inaction: a guard under fire who can't move says he has nowhere to go. Every decision about what to say is made after the squad behaviour has decided what to do — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- **Blacklist:**
  - **Three-tier barks:** very specific lines first, then generic ones, then fully generic, so the player learns what he did (that running makes footstep noise) and never hears a memorable line twice.
  - **Conversations** are group behaviours, with custom breakout and rejoin lines and dialogue blocked out so it can resume.
  - **Hearing fairness:** off-screen NPCs far enough away that not hearing is plausible have their hearing halved for certain events, also applied to some indirect visual events such as seeing an NPC get shot. The creative director stopped complaining about unfair detections.
  - **Sound distance:** computed over the area graph (source to the nearest choke of each area along the area path, then to the listener), replacing an expensive and bug-prone sound-engine query.

  [Walsh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf)
- **Thief:** knowledge spreads between AIs through speech and observation, with controls that limit how far it cascades across a level. Sounds carry semantic tags, propagate through level geometry, arrive from the correct direction, and carry attenuated awareness; spoken concepts can carry information from other AIs — [Leonard 2003](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/building-an-ai-sensory-system-examining-the-design-of-i-thief-the-dark-project-i-)

### Inferences
- **Radio check-in as two scheduled events:**
  1. The operator calls post X (it can be a percept for anyone near X's radio).
  2. A reply is scheduled after a delay drawn from X's stream, but only if X can act: alive, not held up, not mid-fight.

  The operator's belief changes only on the reply or on a timeout, scheduled with `ctx.schedule` (rule 12). An unanswered call becomes a "post X silent" belief and a commander task "check on X" (MGS2). Everything is plain data: `{post, callTick, deadlineTick, answered}`. If the operator is dead, check-ins stop. That absence is itself a slower cue the commander can notice (Blacklist's silence rule, applied to the radio).
- **Interruptible broadcasts** (MGSV): a radio report is an action with a duration (keying the radio, speaking), during which killing the speaker cancels the scheduled delivery. That gives Lab E's "interrupt an alarm" test a second route besides intercepting the run to the alarm box.
- **Voice versus radio:** voice reaches guards within a hearing radius, computed the Blacklist way over the area or chokepoint graph or the grid, and arrives at once. Radio is camp-wide with a delay and only reaches guards holding radios. Both should land as percepts with a `viaRadio` flag and a source, so brains never read another guard's mind directly. Thief's cascade limits suggest a hop count or a confidence decay per relay.
- **Explaining the AI** (F.E.A.R., Blacklist): pick voice lines after the decision, as two-guard exchanges where possible, with tiered specificity. The line choice can use the speaker's own stream, so audio variety never touches others' decisions; and if speech doesn't change simulation state, it can be chosen outside the simulation. Rule 14 (what is drawn is what is simulated) only binds lines that affect outcomes, such as being heard.

### Gaps
- No primary source (Konami or Kojima Productions) for MGSV's command-post check-in schedule, unanswered-call handling or call durations; Metal Gear Wiki pages returned HTTP 402 and further searches could not be run. Treat the MGSV mechanics above as player-reported.
- No published radio-delay values from any shipped game were found.
- Unanswered check-ins in other games (for example Hitman, Ghost Recon, Sniper Ghost Warrior) were not searched.

## 5. Memory of incidents and adaptation within a mission (and briefly across missions)

### Takeaway
Shipped games adapt within a mission in three ways:
- **Lowering detection thresholds once the enemy has met the player.** The Last of Us's threshold drops permanently after an NPC first perceives the player.
- **Escalating stages that change equipment and posture.** Splinter Cell's alarm stages; Wildlands' patrol level; Sniper Elite 3's per-shot escalation.
- **Context memory that changes how a stimulus is read and blocks repeats.** Blacklist's "back on the rooftop after a massacre", group behaviours that don't repeat, investigation cooldowns, and Guild Wars 2's runtime and cooldown considerations.

Alien: Isolation unlocks new behaviours over time but never in response to player deaths. MGSV's Revenge System is the cross-mission version, documented only by fans and guides: helmets after many headshots, night-vision goggles after repeated night operations.

### Cited Findings
- **The Last of Us:** the perception threshold is much higher until an NPC has perceived the player at least once in its lifetime, and much lower in combat. The visibility raycast point moves from the chest (stealth) to the top of the head (combat), favouring the player in stealth — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Blacklist contextual memory:** the same event means different things in different contexts. A body found during a search causes agitation; the same body in a war zone is ignored. Seeing the player on a rooftop again after he massacred five NPCs from it leads to a callout and a run to safety rather than engagement. Group behaviours check that certain behaviours aren't repeated, the disappearing-NPC investigation has a long cooldown to prevent exploits, and barks degrade from specific to generic with repetition — [Walsh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf)
- **Guild Wars 2:** runtime considerations make a decision stop being chosen after a short time or a number of repetitions; cooldowns hold its score near zero until a delay has passed, then let it return — [Lewis](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- **Alien: Isolation:** locked behaviours unlock as conditions are met, so the alien seems to learn. Player deaths are never an unlock trigger, and campaign checkpoints unlock any behaviour still locked — [Thompson](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation) **[secondary; the article does not name the triggers]**
- **Thief:** alertness feeds back into the senses (for example, which view cones are relevant), so an alerted guard perceives differently — [Leonard 2003](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/building-an-ai-sensory-system-examining-the-design-of-i-thief-the-dark-project-i-)
- **Halo 2:** props keep per-object, per-behaviour history (when last meleed, search and pathfinding failures), so a target that defeated a search is treated differently later — [Isla 2005](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- **F.E.A.R.:** knowledge of obstacles that invalidated a plan is written to working memory and used when re-planning — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- **Splinter Cell alarm stages:** each alarm moves guards to a new level of awareness and equipment (flak jackets and helmets in Pandora Tomorrow; four stages in Chaos Theory) — [Splinter Cell Wiki: Alarm](https://splintercell.fandom.com/wiki/Alarm) **[low confidence: fan wiki]**
- **Sniper Elite 3:** repeated unmasked shots from the same spot escalate the enemy response, ending in the exact position revealed — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-07-11-sniper-elite-3-review-war-and-pieces.html) **[secondary]**
- **Left 4 Dead:** a mob's size grows from a minimum just after one spawned to a maximum later, to balance successive mobs. Boss events never repeat back to back — [Booth 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- **MGSV Revenge System (across missions)** [shipped, 2015]: enemy preparedness tracks the player's habits. Many headshots lead to helmets; repeated night infiltrations lead to night-vision goggles; one guide claims heavy tranquilizer use leads to gas masks (uncorroborated). Combat-deployment missions sabotage the supply of specific equipment and lower preparedness. Points are reportedly earned from actions such as killing during combat, with values that differ between sources — [Metal Gear Wiki: Revenge System](https://metalgear.fandom.com/wiki/Revenge_System_(enemy_preparedness)) (via search summary; the page returned 402); [SegmentNext](https://segmentnext.com/mgsv-phantom-pain-enemies-response-system-defense-vehicles-guide/) **[low confidence: fan wiki and guides]**

### Inferences
- **Raised baseline after an incident:** model it as a per-guard and per-camp floor on awareness decay (Thief's capacitor with a floor), plus a permanent threshold drop for any guard who has positively perceived the player once (The Last of Us). That matches the design doc's "settles one level above where it started". The floor and multipliers are tuning numbers (rule 4).
- **Repeated tricks losing effect:** keep a per-camp or per-guard "habituation" ledger keyed by stimulus kind and location, for example `{kind: 'thrownStone', cell, count, lastTick}`. Each repeat lowers that stimulus's Curious utility and raises the chance the guard checks the *source* rather than the landing point. GW2's runtime and cooldown curves, and Blacklist's "don't repeat this group behaviour" flags and long cooldowns, are the shipped analogues. All of it is plain data and percept-derived, so it stays within the boundary.
- **Context reinterpretation** (Blacklist rooftop): store incident records in camp memory (`{kind: 'shotsFrom', perchId, casualties, tick}`). Contextual DSE considerations ("perch with a history of casualties") make guards take cover, call the marksman, or avoid open ground facing that perch rather than walk into its sightline. That gives counter-sniper behaviour that visibly learns within a mission.
- **Escalation stages that change posture** (Splinter Cell, Wildlands): tie Uneasy, Alert and Lockdown to visible changes (paired patrols, helmets on, searchlights, more guards on posts, the target near the vehicle). Players can then read the camp's memory, which helps Lab E's "explain every reaction" criterion.
- **Unlocks never from failures** (Alien): if behaviours unlock within a mission, unlock them from incidents the player caused and can see (a body found, a shot heard), never from his deaths or retries. Otherwise the debrief can't explain them.

- Hitman's within-mission adaptation (how areas become more alert after incidents, how long it lasts): no source found.
- Splinter Cell Conviction and Blacklist within-mission escalation numbers: none found.
- MGSV's within-mission adaptation (Caution persistence, reinforcement build-up): no primary source.
- No published examples of guards ignoring a specific distraction after repeated use were found beyond the general cooldown and no-repeat mechanisms above.

## 6. Grid navigation for many agents under determinism

### Takeaway
For 5–16 guards on a 2.5 m grid plus a 0.5 m camp grid, plain A* with deterministic tie-breaking and an iteration budget is enough. The published speed-ups matter less than two shipped lessons. First, use two tiers: The Last of Us ran coarse navmesh paths plus a local per-NPC grid, and could afford only one local-grid path per frame, so NPCs queued. Second, tactical paths need costs, not just distance: Killzone added cost for being under fire or crossing friendly lines of fire, and The Last of Us used exposure as a cost. Jump point search is optimal and needs no preprocessing, but only on uniform-cost grids. JPS+ is up to about two orders of magnitude faster than optimized A* in open maps but needs a static map. Flow fields fit "many agents to one goal" (everyone to the alarm, the evacuation). Floating-point determinism problems come from transcendental functions, fused multiply-add, compiler settings and library variance, which Sightline's rules already exclude.

### Cited Findings
- **Jump point search (Harabor & Grastien, AAAI 2011)** [research, since widely used]: for undirected uniform-cost grids. It expands only "jump points", is provably optimal, needs no preprocessing and no memory overhead, and speeds A* up by an order of magnitude or more — [paper](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~dharabor/data/papers/harabor-grastien-aaai11.pdf)
- **JPS+ (Rabin, Game AI Pro 2, 2015)** [technique]:
  - It preprocesses 8 numbers per cell (distances to walls and jump points), so it needs a static uniform-cost map.
  - Speed-up over a highly optimized A*: up to two orders of magnitude in open maps (116× in the worked example), about 2.5× in maze-like maps, and about 20% faster than plain JPS there.
  - Pruning accounts for roughly half of JPS's gain.
  - Tips: preallocate nodes with a dirty bit, use an octile heuristic, and a bucket-sorted open list gives about 10× for 1–10 MB of memory.

  [Rabin](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter14_JPS_Plus_An_Extreme_A_Star_Speed_Optimization_for_Static_Uniform_Cost_Grids.pdf)
- **Flow field tiles (Supreme Commander 2, 2010)** [shipped]: one-way A* paths made units collide and stall, and players babysat them. The fix: the world is split into 10 × 10 m sectors of 1 m squares, joined by portal windows that form a graph. Each sector has an 8-bit cost field (255 marks walls, 1–254 are traversal costs that can represent slopes or swamps; clear sectors share one static field), a 24-bit integration field (16-bit cost to goal plus flags), and a flow field derived from it — [Emerson, Game AI Pro ch. 23](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter23_Crowd_Pathfinding_and_Steering_Using_Flow_Field_Tiles.pdf)
- **The Last of Us two-tier navigation** [shipped]: coarse triangulated navmeshes for long routes, plus a 2D grid around each character with static and dynamic blockers rasterized into it, for short precise paths. It ran 20–40 navmesh pathfinds per frame in about 4 ms of PS3 SPU time, but local-grid pathfinds were expensive enough to allow one per frame with NPCs queued. The exposure map (a bitmap of what the player or any NPC can see, from an 8-bit height map in each navmesh, 360° integer raycasts over several frames, 2–3 ms) is integrated along path edges as an extra cost, giving stealthy routes — [McIntosh](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Killzone tactical pathfinding:** A* link costs are increased for crossing friendly lines of fire and for being under fire from one or more threats, so the tactical path differs from the shortest one — [Beij & Straatman 2005](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/gdce05_killzone_ai.pdf)
- **Killzone 2 path costs:** area costs from an influence map (safe almost free, risky exponentially worse), updated as costs change. Estimates are refined iteratively and greedily and can then be updated incrementally, with the work per update adjustable to trade quality for speed — [Straatman & Champandard 2009](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GAIC09_Killzone2Bots_StraatmanChampandard.pdf)
- **Left 4 Dead path following:** A* over a navmesh gives jagged paths. Path optimization collapses redundant nodes; reactive path following steers toward a look-ahead point further down the path. Reactive following superposes well with mob flocking and looks fluid, but can drift off the path too much — [Booth 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- **Floating-point determinism (Fiedler):** lockstep and replays are deterministic with the same binary, compiler and settings. Divergence comes from x87 80-bit intermediates, transcendental functions (sin, cos and tan differed between AMD and Intel), compiler algebraic rewrites and fused multiply-add, approximate reciprocal and inverse-square-root instructions, solver iteration counts in libraries, and system math libraries. Recommendations: strict IEEE modes, no fast-math, avoid or wrap transcendentals, desync detection tools — [Gaffer on Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/floating_point_determinism/)
- **Open-source libraries** (licenses checked via the GitHub API, Nov 2026):
  - [PathFinding.js](https://github.com/qiao/PathFinding.js): MIT (declared in package.json; GitHub detects no license). Ten finders including A*, Dijkstra, best-first, IDA*, bidirectional variants and JumpPointFinder; only some are guaranteed shortest. Last push 2024.
  - [easystar.js](https://github.com/prettymuchbryce/easystarjs): MIT. Asynchronous by default with an `enableSync` option and `setIterationsPerCalculation` budgets, called from a ticker.
  - [ngraph.path](https://github.com/anvaka/ngraph.path): MIT, graph A* and variants.
  - [l1-path-finder](https://github.com/mikolalysenko/l1-path-finder): MIT, 2D grids, last push 2016.
  - [RVO2](https://github.com/snape/RVO2): ORCA local avoidance in C++. GitHub detects the code license as Apache-2.0; the README states CC BY-SA 4.0 for its text.
  - [Recast Navigation](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation): navmesh toolset, zlib.

### Inferences
- **Plain A* (with fixed tie-breaks)** on 2.5 m and 0.5 m grids is the best default. Break ties by f, then h, then cell index (or a fixed neighbour order), never by insertion into an unstable heap. Cap expansions per search and per tick (rule 18), with an unfinished search's cursor kept in state, or recompute from scratch on a later tick, which is simpler to keep in state. Weighted costs (slope, exposure, danger) rule out JPS and JPS+, which need uniform cost.
- **Two tiers** (The Last of Us): 2.5 m valley paths, then 0.5 m camp-grid refinement near the goal or inside the camp. Hand off at portal cells on the boundary between the grids. Matches the design doc's plan.
- **Tactical costs** (Killzone, The Last of Us exposure): costs must come from the guard's beliefs, such as believed danger cells or a believed shot bearing, never from the player's true position (rule 13). A precomputed static table works too (Killzone's line-of-fire table), giving "exposure from perch P" as part of the world.
- **JPS** fits only if a uniform-cost grid layer exists (for example, a pure walkability grid in the camp). Use it as a pure function: no preprocessing, deterministic. JPS+ fits static grids only. Rebuild it at generation and treat it as part of the world, so generation fingerprints cover it (rule 6).
- **Flow fields** (Supreme Commander 2): fit one-goal, many-agent moves such as everyone converging on the alarm box, rallying at a vehicle, or the evacuation route. One integration field serves all guards. Integer or 8-bit costs are deterministic.
- **Local avoidance:** for 5–16 bodies, Sightline's motor already resolves body-body collision (`resolveMove`). ORCA or RVO2 would be a second collision authority and risks violating rule 10's single-motor principle. If needed, add simple steering inside `MoveIntent` (look-ahead plus separation), as L4D's reactive following does. Keep ORCA as a reference only.
- **Libraries:** PathFinding.js, ngraph.path and l1-path-finder can serve as pure functions inside `src/sim` if they use only allowed math and no randomness (not audited here; check for `Math.random`, transcendental calls and iteration-order assumptions). easystar.js's asynchronous design and hidden per-instance state conflict with rules 2 and 9 unless used strictly synchronously within one call. Yuka, behavior3js and mistreevous keep state in class instances, so their state would need flattening into `sim.state`.
- **Determinism in JavaScript:** `+ - * /` and `Math.sqrt` are correctly rounded IEEE operations, so grid A*, diffusion and greedy assignment are bit-identical across Chrome, Firefox, WebKit, Node and Bun. The transcendental hazard Fiedler names is the one CLAUDE.md already forbids. This is an inference from the IEEE requirements, not verified here, but consistent with the project's own cross-engine checks.
