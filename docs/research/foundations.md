# Make fairness a test, not a feeling

Sightline can go past the best of every lane by doing what no studio in this research could: treat its own deterministic, recorded, self-explaining simulation as the answer key, so that every claim about readability, fairness or adaptation becomes a property checked against what the simulation knows happened. The teams behind today's strongest practice worked from outside their games (Bungie and Valve watched testers, Klei rebuilt an alarm players called random, Riot spent nearly a year retrofitting determinism), and every adaptive enemy and pacing director found here reads the truth somewhere it shouldn't, from MGSV counting headshots nobody witnessed to The Last of Us's 5 m shortcut toward the player's real position. Measured this way, the current plan has two weak points to fix first: lab A's exit test passes a tester whose true accuracy is exactly 80% only **57%** of the time, yet passes an owner who answers the clear trials from the rules and guesses every borderline one about **18%** of the time; and five lab branches are about to move shared goldens under landing rules that are right but enforced by hand. The research found no shipped game that draws patrol routes only from what the player watched, and no published test of how well players predict detection, which leaves Sightline room to lead with a debrief that sets what the player knew beside what each guard knew, a director proven honest by the same no-omniscience test the guards face, and a renderer whose agreement with the simulation is measured in CI. For the many games and modes to come, Frostbite's failure and Overwatch's hard-won rules point the same way: keep the shared kernel narrow, and enforce its contracts by test rather than by vigilance. The engineering core already matches or beats industry practice, so most of the work is sharpening: five cheap seams in Wave 0, a stronger trial protocol and honesty tests inside the labs, a knowledge model and a comparative debrief in Phase 2, and later, doctrine that learns only from evidence and a verified daily contract.

## One move tops every lane: let the simulation grade itself

Prior art in all seven lanes is largely a set of workarounds for one handicap: teams could not see inside their own games while people played them. Studios inferred understanding from behaviour and tuned forgiveness by feel. They hid adaptation, because exposing it would expose the cheat, and they hunted desyncs through **50 MB message traces** ([Bettner & Terrano](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/1500-archers-on-a-28-8-network-programming-in-age-of-empires-and-beyond)). Sightline's working rules remove that handicap. The simulation stands apart from the renderer (rule 1) and replays bit for bit from recorded input (rules 2 and 5). It keeps all state in one hashed tree (rule 9), explains every event back to its causes (rule 8 and `explain(id)`), and lets a guard act only on what reached his mind (rule 13). So the strongest move is the same in every lane: compute what others had to infer, test what others tuned by feel, and show truthfully what others had to hide. The table maps the seven lanes, and the sections after it give the evidence.

| Lane | Strongest known practice | Where it falls short for Sightline | Where Sightline can go beyond |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stealth and systemic design | Legible rules with perceivable consequences, and escalation that costs options rather than lives (Looking Glass, Klei, Mimimi, IOI) | Multiple solutions were built by hand; vision cones can't be drawn in first person; sniping makes AI look oblivious | The simulation, not designers, certifies each generated camp's approaches |
| Playtesting and user research | Watch what players do, fix and retest fast (Valve, RITE); forced-choice risk judgements (McGill) | No published test of detection prediction; one expert tester; unreliable agent and VLM judges | Statistically sound exit tests, scored against the simulation's record of every cue |
| Missions, scoring, debrief, daily | Commitment points, flat "perfect" bonuses, death recaps, replays you can take over, one-seed dailies | Dailies unverifiable, or verifiable only for legality; maps leak live intel; debriefs show what happened, not why | A model of what the player observed, set beside each guard's mind |
| Adaptation and pacing | MGSV's revenge ledger, XCOM 2's capped Dark Events, Left 4 Dead's phased director, The Last of Us's coordinator | Each reads ground truth or the player's state somewhere | Doctrine and a director that read only what the faction perceived, proven by test |
| Rendering and performance | One definition of "lit" (The Dark Mod), rim-lit silhouettes (TF2), settings parity (PUBG) | Shadow maps can't match a raycast; LOD and fog add motion; a guard at 300 m is 6 px tall | "Drawn equals simulated" measured in CI, with no renderer-made motion in the far field |
| Determinism and testing | Lockstep checksums, rewind-every-frame checks (GGPO, Factorio), canonical CI scenes (Box2D) | Built for one team's code, not five parallel branches moving shared goldens | Synctest, bisection down to the state path, and per-lane goldens as standing tools |
| Architecture for many games | Narrow kernels extracted from shipped games (ICE, Snowdrop); declared order and access (Bevy, Overwatch) | Frostbite-style mandates; ECS storage wins depend on native memory layout | Declared data access and lane contracts enforced by tests, plus statistical behaviour gates |

One caveat applies throughout. The research behind this report ran out of search budget partway through. Several primary talks were seen only as abstracts or secondary summaries: Martin Walsh's on Blacklist's perception, IOI's on its analytics and Rare's on Sea of Thieves' testing. Many shipped numbers come from datamines, mod repositories and community wikis, and they are flagged where a conclusion rests on them. Where this report says something has not been done, it means only that the research found no example.

## Postmortems agree on legible rules, and warn that sniping makes guards look dumb

**The strongest practice.** The immersive sim rests on two ideas Doug Church named in 1999. The first is intention, the player's ability to form and carry out a plan; the second is perceivable consequence, the world's clear reaction to it ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/formal-abstract-design-tools)). Warren Spector's Deus Ex postmortem turns them into level rules: obstacles with several solutions, goals visible before the player reaches them, and real-world logic so outcomes can be predicted ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-ion-storm-s-i-deus-ex-i-)). Mimimi made information a pillar of Desperados III, even at realism's expense, so that players can always see why a plan failed ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-real-time-stealth-and-combat-of-i-desperados-iii-i-)). For "being seen starts a problem, not a game over", the best model is Klei's. Invisible, Inc.'s first alarm had 20–30 steps, and players could neither read when the next one would matter nor predict its consequences. The fix was **six levels of five silent sub-steps each, every level with a named systemic effect that a tooltip previews**, plus renaming ALARM to SECURITY LEVEL ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-alarm-systems-in-klei-s-i-invisible-inc-i-)). IOI tuned forgiveness by test, and its testers preferred a **20-second stand-down to a 50-second one** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-tightrope-walk-i-hitman-absolution-i-freedom-and-realism)). Mark of the Ninja put checkpoints between nearly every encounter, because sparse retries make patience tedious ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-mark-of-the-ninja-i-s-five-stealth-design-rules)). Teardown leaves preparation untimed and starts a 60-second clock only when the player takes the first target ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/combining-bombastic-heists-with-a-fully-destructible-voxel-world-in-i-teardown-i-); [Teardown docs](https://get-teardown.readthedocs.io/en/latest/gameplay/heist-basics.html)). Underneath it all is Thief, whose guards decided only from what they could perceive and used sound in both directions ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-i-thief-the-dark-project-i-)).

**Where it falls short.** The documented failures land squarely on a sniper sandbox. Bungie's Halo playtests found that **sniping made the AI look oblivious**. Fewer than a third of testers linked Grunts fleeing to the death of their leader, even after the team exaggerated it. A deliberate perception delay read as a bug, so the team removed it. The talk's rule was "If it isn't totally obvious, it's too subtle" ([Bungie](https://www.halo.bungie.org/misc/gdc.2002.haloai/talk.html?page=19)). The same tests showed that **toughness reads as intelligence**. By the slide numbers, as far as they can be read, 43% of testers rated tough enemies very intelligent, against about 8% for weak ones ([Bungie slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)). A readability test run at easy settings will therefore judge the guards dumb. Range dominance is the genre's recurring degenerate strategy. It runs from Far Cry 2's long-range rifles ([The Ringer](https://theringer.com/video-games/2023/10/20/23924419/far-cry-2-15th-anniversary-clint-hocking-ubisoft-open-world-fps)) to Sniper Elite 3, where a reviewer found that the 30 m relocation prompt turned sniping into a repetitive loop of shooting, moving and setting up again ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-07-11-sniper-elite-3-review-war-and-pieces.html)). The methods do not transfer either. Deus Ex brute-forced its multiple paths by hand and let special-case scripting creep in. Desperados' readability depends on drawn vision cones that a first-person honest view cannot show. And IOI's own metrics say **most players restart once a level turns into a large shootout** ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/hitman-turns-25-retrospective-interview)), so a Loud grade means something only if loud play stays interesting.

**Beyond it.** Sightline can certify what Deus Ex had to brute-force. Spelunky lays out a guaranteed solution path before it places a single obstacle ([Kazemi](https://tinysubversions.com/spelunkyGen/)). A camp generator can do the same: lay out the three required approaches and the interceptable escape first, then prove them in space and time against the routines. McGill's RRT tool showed this is computable for stealth paths ([Tremblay et al.](https://www.sable.mcgill.ca/~clump/papers/tremblay-13-exploration.pdf)). Safe-interval path planning keeps A*'s guarantees while collapsing time into a few safe intervals per cell ([SIPP](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/sipp-safe-interval-path-planning-for-dynamic-environments)), which suits it to certifying infiltration. Patrolling theory's worst idleness, the longest a cell goes unwatched ([Machado et al.](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/mabs/MachadoRZD02.html)), gives a precise measure of each window a recon player hunts for. IOI's Zi Peters found that problems appear only once targets and their patrol loops are placed, and he designs loops instead of dead ends ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/from-pen-and-paper-to-blockouts-how-level-designers-craft-iconic-stealthy-encounters)). Validation should therefore run after staffing and require an escape loop for every infiltration route. Opportunities can come from the camp's own systems: a worker refuelling the escape vehicle, a generator that cycles, a post about to miss its check-in. If they reach the player only as things observed, they never harden into a walkthrough.

Range dominance needs three independent guards. Generation sees to it that no single perch wins. Beliefs drive the counter-sniper response from each guard's estimate of the shot's direction, not from a fixed relocation radius, which is where Sniper Elite 4 went after Sniper Elite 3 ([Player.One](https://player.one/sniper-elite-4-dev-outlines-many-ways-se4-will-improve-upon-its-predecessor-544309)). Scoring makes sure a bot that only snipes from maximum range finishes Loud, never Ghost. Two tests would make the pillars falsifiable. One is an **intention check**: the tester states a plan after recon, and the replay shows whether it unfolded as stated. The other is to **run every readability test at target difficulty**. With no saves inside a mission, the retry loop is the whole mission. Retrying a seed should be near-instant and keep the player's pins and notes, so that knowledge becomes the progression. Each camp level should preview its next consequence in the fiction and on the map, Klei's tooltip fix applied to Calm, Uneasy, Alert and Lockdown. How often players quit after an alarm should be tracked, since that is IOI's warning sign. Halo's lesson also becomes a hard requirement for lab E. A guard shot at or startled from 300 m must react visibly, by diving, pointing or radioing, or the honest view will read as oblivious. F.E.A.R. used paired radio exchanges to make its squads' coordination visible, but it also faked a reinforcement call that had no mechanic behind it ([Orkin](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)). Sightline's barks can copy the exchanges and never the fake. Creative Assembly's readable vocal language for its alien took nearly the whole development cycle ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/retrospective/2025/06/24/how-creative-assembly-built-its-perfectly-evolved-ai-xenomorph)). That argues for designing barks as a vocabulary from lab A onward, rather than treating them as placeholders until the Sound track.

## Lab A's 80% bar is a coin flip at 80% and passable without reading cues

**The strongest practice.** Valve's playtesting treats designs as hypotheses and playtests as experiments. It trusts what players do over what they say, because people cannot reliably report why they act as they do ([Valve](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ValvesApproachToPlaytesting.pdf)). Microsoft's RITE method fixes a problem as soon as one participant shows it, then keeps testing the revised build ([MeasuringU](https://measuringu.com/rite-method/)). In cued retrospective reporting, participants narrate over a replay of their own actions, which recovers more of the problem-solving process than plain recall ([van Gog et al.](https://research.ou.nl/en/publications/uncovering-the-problem-solving-process-cued-retrospective-reporti/)). The closest published precedent for lab A is a McGill study in which 27 people picked the safer of two animated stealth paths. Their consensus reached 75% on only **10 of 15 levels**, and a plain distance-to-guards metric matched the consensus best ([Tremblay et al.](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~jtremb59/Papers/fdg2014_paper_33.pdf)). The research found **no published study of how accurately players predict detection outcomes**, in any genre. Lab A's test is therefore more rigorous than common practice, and it must stand on its statistics rather than on precedent.

**Where it falls short.** Those statistics are weaker than they look. The table below was computed for this report with exact binomial sums. It assumes independent trials, and a pass means at least 48 of 60 overall and at least 21 of 30 in each sitting. On those terms the gate behaves like a one-sided test that true accuracy exceeds 70%, not a test of 80%:

| Owner's true accuracy | Chance the gate passes |
| --- | --- |
| 70% | 5.6% |
| 75% | 22.9% |
| 80% | 57.3% |
| 85% | 89.2% |
| 90% | 99.4% |

A score of exactly 48 out of 60 shows accuracy above about 70% (the one-sided 95% Wilson bound is 70.3%). Showing 80% at that confidence takes about **54 out of 60** (intervals as recommended by [Brown, Cai & DasGupta](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.ss/1009213286)). The per-sitting floor also punishes learning: a tester who climbs from 65% to 95% between sittings averages 80% but passes only 35% of the time. The larger problem is validity. If half the trials are clear and half borderline, an owner who answers the clear half from the sight table and guesses on every borderline trial still passes **about 18%** of the time. One who reaches 60% on the borderline trials passes 58% of the time. The plan's balance rule (half the trials end in detection, half are borderline) can also be read as a partition, and on that reading answering "yes" every time beats 50%. Testers differ widely. In Faulkner's simulations, five-user samples found 85% of problems on average but as few as 55% ([Faulkner](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14587545/)). The owner is an unusual draw who cannot forget the rules he wrote. The other human checks are loose too. Zero unexplained reactions out of 20 in lab E only bounds the true rate below 13.9% (95%); out of 60, below 4.9%. Lab D's spot check of 3 of 10 camps catches a single bad camp 30% of the time.

Automated testers do not close the gap on their own, and each kind fails differently:

| Tester | Good at | Bad at | Best use in Sightline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scripted and invariant bots (today's) | Regressions, determinism, unwinnable seeds | Unknown unknowns | Rules 10–11, lab D's validation, lab E's scenarios |
| Search over the simulation (RRT, safe-interval search, MCTS personas) | Proving a plan exists, sampling plans, risk maps | Knows more than a player unless limited to recon information | Lab D's best-plan search; generating lab A's borderline trials |
| Reinforcement learning and competitive agents | Coverage and exploits | Training cost, inhuman play | Hunting loopholes in the motor and perception |
| LLM agents on text | Ranking relative difficulty | Unverified plans; position and verbosity bias | Lab D's reviewer, checked by simulation |
| Vision-language agents on frames | Gross visibility checks | Timing, fine detail, overlays | Flagging missing cues only |

Behind the table: MCTS personas evolved to match different decision styles play the same level differently ([Holmgård et al.](https://antoniosliapis.com/papers/automated_playtesting_with_procedural_personas_through_mcts_with_evolved_heuristics.pdf)). EA SEED's reward-driven agents raised test coverage and surfaced exploits ([Bergdahl et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15819)). OpenAI's hide-and-seek agents, in a world of boxes and ramps, worked through six phases of strategy, including press-reported uses of the physics nobody intended ([Baker et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.07528)). Rovio found that an agent's best-case performance predicted human pass rates better than its average ([Roohi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12061v1)). GPT-4 ranked Wordle and Slay the Spire difficulty at correlations of 0.62 to 0.87 with human data while playing below human level ([Xiao & Yang](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02829v1)). Frontier vision-language models completed **0.48%** of a real-time benchmark of 1990s games ([VideoGameBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18134v2)), and the best of 11 models caught 43.4% of known glitches ([GlitchBench](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Taesiri_GlitchBench_Can_Large_Multimodal_Models_Detect_Video_Game_Glitches_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)). VLM judges barely changed their scores when video was shuffled or frozen, yet scored higher when text overlays were added ([Kim et al.](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/175831)). Timing and overlay-like cues are exactly what lab A's pre-screen would rely on. LLM judges carry position and verbosity biases ([Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)), and a developer survey found studios sceptical of automated agents, naming the test oracle as the missing piece ([Politowski et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12777)).

**Beyond it.** Sightline already has the oracle. The simulation knows which cue fired, where and when: an executable answer key, the kind a 2026 study used to hold VLM judges to ground truth ([Wang](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/172984)). The strongest version of the A and C protocol follows from that. Stratify each sitting two ways, detected or not and clear or borderline, with seven or eight trials in each of the four cells. Score balanced accuracy, the mean of the hit rate and the correct-rejection rate. Compute a **rules-only baseline** for every trial set, the sight table applied to distance, stance and light. Tremblay's result says this baseline will be strong, so require the owner to beat it on the borderline trials. Give those trials their own bar of at least 21 of 30, which chance passes 2.1% of the time and a true 75% reader passes 80%. If the claim is readability at first sight, hold outcomes back until a scored sitting ends. Check that no surface feature, shade for instance, gives away which trials are borderline. Spread the trials with a pairwise covering array over the seven situation factors. That borrows NIST's finding that one or two factors trigger 96% of software faults ([NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/Projects/automated-combinatorial-testing-for-software/Our-Research-Program)); it is an analogy, not a perception result. A one-tap "which cue told you?" probe, compared with the top factor from `explain(id)`, diagnoses without ever gating. Having the owner narrate misses over the bit-exact replay gives cued retrospection for free. Pooling answers across sittings allows item analysis. **Cue lead time**, from the first visible cue to detection, should be tracked as a distribution with its worst cases, since Valve warns that averages hide extremes. Between gate sittings, iterate with a sequential probability ratio test, Stockfish's method for accepting engine changes ([Fishtest](https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/fishtest-wiki/Fishtest-Mathematics.html)), while keeping the gate itself fixed. Agents should first pass a perception check on frames with known answers. After that, let them drop only trials whose cue the simulation shows to be absent, occluded or under a pixel threshold, so the scored set never drifts toward what agents can read.

Lab D needs the same rigour. Replace "no two signatures match" with signatures drawn from a closed vocabulary that count as a match on three of four fields, and execute every reviewer's plan in the simulation. Use several reviewers who see the camps in shuffled order. Add a **cross-camp regret matrix**: camp i's best plan, applied to camp j, must do clearly worse than j's own best. Puzzle generators already search the whole space of play to reject levels with undesirable shortcut solutions ([Smith, Butler & Popović](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/smith2013qop.pdf)). Restricted-play agents measure how much a game depends on one strategy ([Jaffe et al.](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12513/12513-52-16035-1-2-20201228.pdf)), so a bot that only snipes is a direct test of "no single perch wins". The owner's pass is necessary but not sufficient. Three to five naive testers before Concept proven would show whether a player who never saw the sight table can read the same cues.

## The debrief can set what you knew beside what each guard knew

**The strongest practice.** IOI found that permanence changes how the same content is played: Elusive Target players took more care with witnesses. It reconciled scouting with permanence through a **Rubicon rule**: restart freely until the first kill or objective, never after it ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-elusive-targets-system-in-2016-s-i-hitman-i-)). In Hitman's Freelancer mode, by players' accounts, alerting the network sends the leader running for an exit ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1659040/discussions/0/598518216964213750)). Scoring that rewards the hard thing uses flat bonuses for a perfect run that dwarf the points for single actions. MGSV's datamined table pays **100,000 points for No Traces, against 200 per enemy neutralized**, and caps the rank at A when an assist item is used ([MGSV Modding Wiki](https://mgsvmoddingwiki.github.io/TppResult.lua)). Far Cry 3 paid about 550 XP for an outpost taken without an alarm and 1,500 for one taken undetected ([GamerGuides](https://www.gamerguides.com/far-cry-3/introduction/gameplay/outposts)), even when an animal did the killing ([Prima](https://primagames.com/eguides/far-cry-3-with-interactive-map-eguide/essentials/key-locations/outposts)). Hitman 3's Silent Assassin rating survives if you destroy the camera footage ([DBLTAP](https://dbltap.com/posts/hitman-3-silent-assassin-requirements-01exd43krjht)). The fastest answers to "why did I fail" index the key moments in advance. League of Legends' 2019 death recap shows what killed you and which abilities to watch for ([ONE Esports](https://www.oneesports.gg/league-of-legends/here-is-your-first-look-at-the-new-league-of-legends-death-recap-screen/)). Counter-Strike 2's top-down replays show positions, thrown utility and a timed kill feed ([op.gg](https://op.gg/cs2/2d-replay)). Tekken 8 lets you **take control from the moment of a tip** and try alternatives ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/tekken-8-overhauls-my-replay-and-tips-allows-for-real-time-practice)). Spelunky's daily proved the format of one shared seed and one attempt, which turned the main game into practice ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/80294/spelunky-pc-introduces-daily-challenges); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-understated-genius-of-the-i-spelunky-i-daily-challenge)). Doom demos and TASVideos have verified runs for decades by re-simulating input recordings on a stated build ([TASVideos](https://tasvideos.org/MovieRules)), with tool-assisted runs ranked separately ([Doom Wiki](https://doom.fandom.com/wiki/Speedrun)).

**Where it falls short.** In a deterministic seed, a restart before the Rubicon is free practice, because patrol timing is identical on every run. One-shot pressure also hides content. IOI's Eskil Mohl said in 2026 that fear of failure keeps players from seeing most of what a mission offers, and IOI now releases its celebrity targets as replayable assignments ([Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/hitman-world-of-assassination/snoop-dogg-interview)). Count-based bonuses let volume back in (thirty MGSV headshots at 1,000 points each), and a bonus for total time punishes recon. Progression multipliers such as the one in Hitman 2's Sniper Assassin mode make scores incomparable ([TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/a263280/peak-performance-achievement)). Freelancer's co-directors conceded that the rewards of its Hardcore mode may not match its difficulty ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/hitman-freelancer-qa-ioi-devs-talk-about-designing-the-game-mode-its-reception-upon-release-and-more/)). Most shipped stealth maps show marked enemies live, through walls. Splinter Cell: Conviction's last-known-position silhouette is the best-known way to show what an enemy believes, but critics found its AI fixated on the silhouette, which made flanking easy ([Expert Reviews](https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/software/28364/splinter-cell-conviction-review)). Spelunky's daily kept no replays, split its seeds by platform ([Spelunky Wiki](https://spelunky.fandom.com/wiki/Daily_Challenge_Mode_%28HD%29)) and let players watch others before their own attempt ([The Ludite](https://theludite.com/2014/06/23/spelunky-when-scoring-goes-bad/)). Most serious for a ranked daily: for years, Trackmania's validation re-simulated input replays faithfully while some players slowed the game down to record. Investigators caught them with input statistics across some 39,000 scraped replays ([donadigo & Wirtual](https://donadigo.com/tmx1)). Re-simulation proves a run is legal, not that a human played it. And the research found no shipped game that draws patrol routes from what the player actually watched.

**Beyond it.** The missing piece is a model of what the player knows, built with the same rigour as `GuardMind`. Make it a **knowledge ledger** inside the simulation. The player becomes one more observer, run through lab A's exposure function at the honest view's angular acuity. That acuity is set in tuning at a reference resolution, so the ledger never varies by machine. The map then shows exactly what was resolvable on screen and nothing the player could not have seen. Its inputs (eye pose, look direction and optic) already arrive as recorded input, so it replays exactly. Guards never read it, which the perception-boundary test can enforce. A route draws itself only along the segments the player watched, each stamped with the time it was seen. Its timing counts as confirmed after two loops, and marks fade with age.

Because both minds are recorded, the debrief becomes a comparison: *you never saw the marksman; he had your rough position from 06:41*. The failure card walks the chain of causes backward. The target evacuated because of Lockdown, Lockdown because a named guard reached the alarm box, and he went because of a sighting, which had its own stimulus. Each link is a recorded event with a jump to its tick. Taking over from any key moment needs only a save-state restore, and its recording still starts at the mission start, because its first ticks are the original inputs. Overlay the route the player sketched on the path actually taken. Give every briefing claim a source and an age, and score each one as confirmed, corrected or never checked.

Scoring follows the same principle. Grades depend on what the camp ends up knowing, so stopping the runner keeps a run Clean. Ghost and Clean are fixed amounts an order of magnitude above any count-based term. Precision is scored as rates, plus the best shot weighted by difficulty. Efficiency runs from first shot to exfil, so watching longer never costs points, and every line item is published. Sniper Elite decides how elaborate its kill cam is from a hidden score of difficulty, distance and target movement ([MCV](https://mcvuk.com/?p=23739)). In Sightline that payoff belongs in the debrief, rebuilt truthfully from the recording; showing it mid-mission would reveal whether the shot killed. Shadow Tactics revealed its optional badges only after a mission's first clear ([GamePro](https://www.gamepro.de/artikel/shadow-tactics-blades-of-the-shogun-die-rueckkehr-der-echtzeit-taktik,3317431,seite2.html)), which keeps a first attempt unbiased and gives the replays after it a purpose. If Hardline ships, it should be rewarded in kind, with a mark on the grade, rather than with gear that will never feel proportionate.

The daily contract should build on all of this. Lock the ranked attempt at insertion, and count quitting as an attempt, as Hitman's Elusive Target Arcade does ([Gamepur](https://gamepur.com/?p=283843)). Issue the seed with a server token so it cannot be practised offline, and hide others' replays until you have submitted. Re-simulate every submission on the build pinned for that day, with its embedded tuning. Run statistics on the inputs to check that a human played, keep every recording so new detectors can run over old records, and rank tool-assisted runs on their own board. Slay the Spire's daily layers a few modifiers over a fixed setup, and by community accounts only the first run of the day counts ([Slay the Spire Wiki](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Custom_Mode); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2868840/discussions/0/591810592556252896/)). Sightline's fog, night or a doctrine preset could make each daily distinct without new content.

## Every director studied here cheats somewhere; Sightline can build an honest one

**The strongest practice.** MGSV's revenge system is the most complete shipped ledger of player habits. It was read from the game script in a mod repository and is believed to match the shipped values. It tracks twelve habits at 100 points per level, adding **5 points per headshot**. Each helmet level puts helmets on another 10% of soldiers, up to 70%, under ceilings tied to story progress. Fourteen dispatch missions each block one gear type for three sorties, and each is offered only while its countermeasure is active. Six threat axes are always visible on the map ([TppRevenge.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TinManTex/InfiniteHeaven/master/tpp/data1_dat-lua-ih/Assets/tpp/script/lib/TppRevenge.lua)). By community accounts, XCOM 2 keeps about three enemy Dark Events pending, and a guerrilla operation can counter one of them ([Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/xcom2/dark-events-game-mechanics/z78546)). Hitman: Blood Money's post-mission newspaper reports what the investigation found ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitman:_Blood_Money)), and Echo's enemies learn only what they saw while the lights were on ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echo_%282017_video_game%29)). For pacing, Left 4 Dead's director builds up, holds a peak for 3–5 s, fades, then enforces a **30–45 s relax**. It changes how often threats come, not how hard they hit ([Booth](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)). Rich Welsh's search coordinator hands out spots from a pool and lets searchers drift back to routine in their own time, because stopping them all at once looks like a hive mind ([Welsh](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter27_Looking_for_Trouble_Making_NPCs_Search_Realistically.pdf)). The Last of Us grants roles from a coordinator and spreads belief across a search grid, clearing each cell a searcher can see ([McIntosh](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)). Halo 3's Objectives system greedily assigns squads to prioritized tasks with capacities ([Halo 3 slides](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)).

**Where it falls short.** Every one of them reads the truth somewhere. MGSV counts a headshot whether or not anyone finds the body. It also has no overall cap and lets its blocks expire. Players complained that the helmets came back, and some simply stopped taking headshots to game the counter ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/287700/discussions/0/1771511442695350520); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/287700/discussions/0/523897653308116986/?ctp=3)). According to Tommy Thompson's analysis, Alien: Isolation's director always knows where the player is and periodically sends the alien toward that area ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)). Welsh adds a subtle pull toward the player's true position to fake intuition. The Last of Us found that its honest search cycle took **nearly two minutes** and felt unresponsive. So NPCs approached at once whenever the player moved more than 5 m from the believed position, which cut the cycle to about 30 s ([McIntosh](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)). Halo 3's assignment cost includes nearness to the player, and Left 4 Dead's intensity measure reads the damage survivors take. Resident Evil 4's hidden rank changed damage and hitboxes. It drew little resentment only because players did not notice it for years ([WhatCulture](https://whatculture.com/gaming/8-video-game-moments-you-didnt-realise-were-tricking-you?page=5)), a protection a game with an honest view gives up.

The research on how players perceive adaptation is blunt. Told that an adaptive AI was running, 21 players reported higher immersion in a world identical to the control (119.9 against 111.9, p = .011) ([Denisova & Cairns](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~pc530/pubs/Denisova2_CHIPLAY15.pdf)). Twelve students facing 22 AIs almost never recognized a strategy, failed to spot a random one 83% of the time and accused a fair one of cheating ([Wetzel & Anderson](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter04_Player_Perception_of_AI_Opponents.pdf)). Monolith found that Shadow of Mordor's best players had the worst time, because its nemesis system fed on player failure ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/upgrading-the-nemesis-system-for-i-middle-earth-shadow-of-war-i-)). MGSV's pace is also far too slow for an operation of five to seven missions: about twenty headshots buy one helmet level, across roughly fifty missions.

**Beyond it.** Sightline's doctrine already sits on the right side of Ernest Adams's line: it strengthens the opposition and never weakens the player ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-designer-s-notebook-difficulty-modes-and-dynamic-difficulty-adjustment)). The goal is adaptation and pacing that are honest by construction and proven honest by test. Doctrine should count only evidence the faction actually has: wounds on bodies it found, shots it heard and the sector they came from, survivors' reports, tools it recovered. Hiding a body then becomes counterplay across missions as well as within one, and the briefing names its evidence: *three sentries found with head wounds, shot from long range*. Klei's preview of the next alarm level, applied to doctrine, becomes a visible board of three slots, plus a fourth habit marked as under consideration. That turns punishment into a forecast. The rest follows from the evidence above. Habits are scored as shares of each mission, like MGSV's zero-sum tally of stealth against combat, rather than as slowly accumulating points; doctrine's strength is capped by mission index; setbacks won through side objectives are permanent rather than expiring; and every blank counterplay cell gets filled. Above all, doctrine never touches perception constants or accuracy, so lab A's prediction test stays valid across a whole operation.

Within a mission, timing and inputs matter most. Hamlet found that changes on stage look erratic, while changes off stage go unnoticed ([Hunicke & Chapman](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/pubs/Hamlet.pdf)). That favours applying doctrine between missions. Klei's experience says posture inside a mission should rise only after incidents the guards perceived, never on a clock. The pacing layer then becomes a coordinator that reads only the faction's own state. The incident's alert class sets a budget of weighted slots, after Dawe's grid weights ([Dawe](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter28_Beyond_the_Kung-Fu_Circle_A_Flexible_System_for_Managing_NPC_Attacks.pdf)). A pool of search spots is seeded from a belief grid, and a relax window bars new convergence after a search. Guards stand down at staggered times drawn from each one's own random stream. A timeout measured from the last contact replaces the 5 m shortcut, and lab E measures search length against The Last of Us's 30 s.

Then prove it. Run the no-omniscience test on the commander and the pacing layer, not only on the guards: two placements of the player beyond every sense must produce identical director decisions. None of the shipped directors above could pass that test. Evaluate blind, with testers unaware whether pacing or doctrine changes are active. Then check whether players can name the active doctrine without the briefing. Wetzel and Anderson predict they mostly cannot, which is what justifies the briefing. Before adding recurring named enemies who remember the player, have counsel read Warner Bros.' nemesis patent, US 10,926,179 ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en)). Faction-level doctrine looks different, but this is a flag, not legal advice.

## Guards are 6 pixels tall at 300 m, so the renderer must add no motion

**The strongest practice.** The Dark Mod made a light's exclusion from the player's light gem and from AI sight a single flag, so the renderer and the AI can never disagree about it ([The Dark Mod](https://bugs.thedarkmod.com/view.php?id=4128)). Thief's team considered judging the player's visibility from the floor's light map, so that places that looked shaded would never trick the player ([Barrett](https://nothings.org/gamedev/thief_rendering.html)). Mark of the Ninja made light binary and drew it plainly ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-mark-of-the-ninja-i-s-five-stealth-design-rules)). For reading enemies at range, Team Fortress 2 emphasized silhouettes with rim highlights, built from a Fresnel term and an up-facing ambient term, rather than outlines ([Mitchell et al.](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall07/cos597B/papers/mitchell-team-fortress.pdf)). Battlefield V built a soldier-visibility system so soldiers stay consistently visible in very dark and very bright scenes ([DICE](https://devtrackers.gg/battlefieldv/p/1567d988-community-broadcast-visibility-improvements)). PUBG made settings parity a rule after its low settings dropped shadows and foliage, and now renders shadows to 450 m even on Low ([Player.One](https://www.player.one/playerunknown-interview-parity-low-high-graphics-battlegrounds-121445)). Static shadows can be rendered once and never shimmer ([three.js](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/LightShadow.html)). Moving cascades shimmer unless they are snapped to texel increments ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/dxtecharts/common-techniques-to-improve-shadow-depth-maps)). Chaos Theory's director argued in 2026 that modern lighting and ambient occlusion lower exactly the contrast players use to tell safe ground from exposed ground ([gamedev.net](https://gamedev.net/news/3499-splinter-cell-designer-says-modern-lighting-has-made-stealth-games-harder-to/)).

**Where it falls short.** Sightline's own numbers make these constraints concrete. They were computed for this report from `tuning.ts` and the renderer. At the 58° vertical view and 1080p, a 1.85 m guard at 300 m is about **6 × 2 pixels**, rising to 42 × 14 at the 9° zoom. At 500 m, unzoomed, he is 3.6 × 1.2. Johnson's criteria put detection at about one line pair, roughly two pixels, across a target's critical dimension ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson%27s_criteria)). The unzoomed guard at 300 m therefore sits right at the detection threshold, and motion carries the read. Any change the renderer itself makes in the far field competes with that cue: shadow shimmer, level-of-detail pops, impostor swaps, z-fighting, or crawl along aliased edges. Several of these are present or latent.

Fog uses view depth (`vDepth = -mv.z` in `src/render/materials.ts`). A guard 500 m away is about 30% fogged at the centre of the screen but 17% at its edge, so turning your head changes his contrast. The 0.08 m near plane and 7 km far plane give a depth step of about 0.75 m at 1 km and 30 m at 6.4 km. That invites z-fighting on edge lines at range, and reversed-Z helps only with a floating-point depth buffer ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/content/depth-precision-visualized)). Stock `THREE.LOD` scales distance by `camera.zoom`, not by field of view. It would pick coarse levels exactly when the player zooms by narrowing `fov` ([three.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r186/src/objects/LOD.js)). Terrain schemes that morph geometry move drawn vertices as the camera moves ([GPU Gems 2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-i-geometric-complexity/chapter-2-terrain-rendering-using-gpu-based-geometry)), which breaks rule 7's single set of triangles. No shadow technique reproduces a point raycast, and height is the bigger trap. Today's light readout samples the player at chest height, about 1.24 m when standing. If lab A's sight keeps that point, a player whose feet are in drawn shade stays lit to guards across the outer **1.8 m** of a shadow under the 35° sun. The band is 1.1 m crouched and 0.34 m prone. WebGPU is no shortcut. three.js still calls its WebGPU renderer experimental and does not support `ShaderMaterial` there ([three.js manual](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/webgpurenderer.html)). Its CPU cost per object is also higher than the WebGL renderer's, even on its WebGL 2 fallback ([three.js issue #30560](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/30560)).

**Beyond it.** The best renderer is one whose honesty is measured. Draw each body lit or shaded from the simulation's own verdict, one uniform per body, which is exact by construction. Draw world shade from a static, sun-fixed shadow map rendered once. Hold both to `lightAt` with a browser test that samples walkable points at foot and chest height. It should require, say, 99.5% agreement, with every disagreement within one texel of a shadow edge, while the debug view overlays the simulation's samples on the drawn shade. If ground shade must agree exactly, the simulation can build a light-space depth grid at generation that both sides compare against; that is a behaviour change for lab A to weigh. The band between feet and chest needs a deliberate call from the owner. He can test several points on the body, drive a small gem from `lightAt` itself, or accept the band and show it.

Set the budget for renderer-made motion in the far field at zero. Fog is computed on radial distance (`length(mvPosition.xyz)`), so turning the head no longer changes a guard's contrast. Nothing inside the playable area or the 500 m shooting band gets level of detail or impostors; the backdrop alone does, chosen by projected size with hysteresis. Depth precision is fixed by a higher near plane or a separate backdrop pass, and antialiasing stays multisampled rather than jittered and temporal. On a figure 6 pixels tall, one-pixel edge lines make up most of the silhouette and crawl with sub-pixel motion, so fade them with projected size. Give every body, guards and player alike, the same TF2-style up-facing rim term, so it never reads as a highlight only guards get. Turn the pixel table into a **cue-legibility budget** that lab A's trial generator enforces. A head turn that spans less than a couple of pixels at its range is not a cue, and the simulation, not an agent, drops any trial that depends on it. Fog belongs in the material, as a gradient keyed to radial distance, height and sun direction; Firewatch's layered colour is the reference ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch)). Drive it from the same tuning as any haze in the guards' sight model, so drawn haze equals simulated haze. Keep PUBG's parity: render scale and antialiasing may change sharpness, never what blocks sight. For the frame budget, web.dev allows about 10 ms of your own work per frame at 60 Hz ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/rendering-performance)). To fit it, turn off `checkShaderErrors` in production and pre-compile with `compileAsync`. Keep `getError` and synchronous `readPixels` out of the frame loop ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API/WebGL_best_practices)). Split instanced vegetation per terrain tile so it can be culled, and move the simulation to a worker only if profiling demands it. Revisit WebGPU at the art pass, and only for a feature that needs it.

## The determinism core already matches or beats industry practice; its gaps sit at the edges

**The strongest practice.** Age of Empires ran every machine from the same commands under per-system checksums, and found that recorded games made perfect repro cases ([Bettner & Terrano](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/1500-archers-on-a-28-8-network-programming-in-age-of-empires-and-beyond)). Riot retrofitted determinism into League of Legends' server over almost a year, which included unifying six clock and timing APIs ([Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/determinism-league-legends-implementation)). It then replayed thousands of real games every day, comparing key-value state logs to find the first divergence. One bug seen in about 1 game in 5,000 turned out to be a single uninitialized vector component ([Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/determinism-league-legends-fixing-divergences)). GGPO's SyncTest rolls back one frame on every frame and compares the re-run ([GGPO](https://github.com/pond3r/ggpo/blob/master/doc/DeveloperGuide.md)), and Factorio's heavy mode saves and loads the game every tick ([Factorio wiki](https://wiki.factorio.com/Desynchronization)). Box2D replaced its trigonometry functions and disabled fused multiply-add. Its CI checks two numbers on every pull request: the steps until every body sleeps (310) and a hash of their transforms ([Box2D](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/08/determinism/)). StarCraft II loads old replays in the matching old build ([Blizzard](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/10495761/update-der-replay-funktion-in-2-0-10)). Planetary Annihilation stored keyframed curves, so its replays did not depend on determinism at all ([Smith](https://www.forrestthewoods.com/blog/tech_of_planetary_annihilation_chrono_cam/)).

**Where it falls short.** Sightline already has most of this by design. It has its own math in `detmath.ts`, a random stream per entity and one hashed plain-data tree with NaN canonicalized. Input is quantized inside the simulation, and recordings are refused with a stated reason. A self-test gives the golden values in Chromium, Firefox, WebKit and Bun, and a weekly hash comparison runs across macOS and Windows. Invariants are checked every tick, and fuzz runs last 40 hours. The tuning validator already rejects -0 and non-finite numbers, and the architecture test already bans `localeCompare`, `toLocale*` and `Intl`.

What remains sits at the edges. ECMAScript specifies `Math.sqrt` only as implementation-approximated ([ECMAScript](https://read262.netlify.app/numbers-and-dates/math-object)); every engine in CI rounds it correctly, but nothing checks an engine outside CI, and engines do change their math, as V8's move of `sin`, `cos` and `pow` onto LLVM's libc shows ([V8](https://github.com/v8/v8/blob/main/src/base/ieee754.cc)). The fuzz run never restores state mid-run (`src/headless/fuzz.ts` has no restore), so only the single 60-second restore test catches state kept outside `sim.state`, or a cache recomputed differently after a load, which caused one of Factorio's deep desyncs ([Factorio](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-340)). Jolt warns against `std::sort` in deterministic code ([Jolt](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html)). Its JavaScript counterpart is an `Array.prototype.sort` comparator that is not a consistent total order with an id tie-break, and no test checks for one yet. A divergence names the part of the state that moved, not the tick and path, and fuzz failures are saved whole rather than minimized. Most pressing, five lab branches are about to move shared goldens. The Phase 1 plan's landing rules are right but enforced by hand, so two branches can each be green and still leave `main` red together. A recording made on a lab's preview deploy also carries the same `SIM_VERSION` as `main` while behaving differently. Because `resim.ts` checks versions, scene, world fingerprint and start hash but not behaviour, it accepts such a recording and then reports a divergence, instead of refusing it up front.

**Beyond it.** Make the vigilance mechanical. A **synctest mode** in `npm run fuzz` would save state every N ticks, step k ticks and restore, through the on-disk codec as well as `structuredClone`. It would then re-step and compare the per-part hashes, combining Factorio's heavy mode, GGPO's SyncTest and Jolt's rewind check ([Jolt](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html)). A **bisect** command would re-simulate from the last matching save-state with a hash every tick, then after each phase within the bad tick. Mortal Kombat's rollback team reportedly ran such fencepost checks, according to a secondary account ([Edgegap](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-mortal-kombat-x-injustice-2-rollback)). It would end by printing a path-level diff such as `guards[3].mind.alert: 0.41 → 0.42`: Riot's tool, made nearly free by the plain-data tree. A minimizer would delta-debug a failing recording down to a two-second repro. Alternatively, bot actions could become fast-check commands, whose shrinking does the same job ([fast-check](https://fast-check.dev/docs/advanced/model-based-testing/)).

Goldens should explain themselves. Beside each moved hash, CI would print the first divergent tick and path, plus a few meaningful numbers, the way Box2D pairs steps-to-sleep with its hash. For Sightline those could be detection times, shots to kill and seeds passing. Each lane would get canonical scenes pinned like goldens, so a lab sees which lane moved. The merged result should be tested before it lands, through a merge queue ([GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/configuring-branches-and-merges-in-your-repository/configuring-pull-request-merges/managing-a-merge-queue)). Where the repository's plan lacks one, a branch rule requiring branches to be up to date with `main` does the same. Rebaselines would be generated by CI inside the pull request and reviewed with it, which is Chromium's practice ([Chromium](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/docs/testing/web_test_expectations.md)). Recordings would carry the pinned behaviour fingerprints of their valley and scene, so a preview recording is refused with a reason. The decisions log, which every bump appends to, would stop conflicting at its tail if it moved to one file per decision or a union merge driver.

Kept playtests should be re-simulated on a schedule, as Riot does with real games. A startup check against known answers for `sqrt` and `detmath` would guard any ranked run. Refused recordings would get a route home. They would open in the deployment that made them, StarCraft II's answer, with a watch-only fallback drawn from the 10 Hz state track. That waits on confirming Vercel's retention of old deployments, which this research could not do.

## Frostbite failed by mandate; Sightline's kernel should win by declared contracts

**The strongest practice.** Shared technology worked when it was extracted from shipped games, stayed narrow and left each team its own gameplay. Sony's ICE team shipped libraries and tools, never one engine ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2013/07/theres_a_secret_room_inside_naughty_dog_thats_as_cold_as_ice)). Snowdrop grew from Massive's shipped engine, with fast iteration as a founding principle, after an earlier engine had taken up to 45 minutes to compile ([Massive](https://massive.se/?p=11738)). MachineGames forked id Tech for Indiana Jones and owned the divergence ([MobyGames](https://mobygames.com/group/20527/3d-engine-motor)). Unreal's Game Features package a feature as data, as actions that inject components into actors that opt in, so unrelated features don't accidentally depend on each other ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/game-features-and-modular-gameplay-in-unreal-engine)). Bevy reports pairs of systems whose data access conflicts when no order is set between them ([Bevy](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy/latest/source/examples/ecs/nondeterministic_system_order.rs)). Overwatch's team spent about a year and a half arriving at its rules: components have no functions, systems have no state, systems never call each other, and one system writes the command that drives the simulation. It then found that its kill cam's second world broke every call site that reached systems through globals ([Sohu write-up of Ford's GDC 2017 talk](https://www.sohu.com/a/148848770_466876)). Photon Quantum, the closest shipped analogue to Sightline, passes one frame holding all state to stateless systems that allocate nothing ([Photon](https://doc.photonengine.com/quantum/current/quantum-intro)). Rare built Sea of Thieves with about 10,000 automated tests in tiers ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026366/Automated-Testing-of-Gameplay-Features)), and Factorio's dependencies between tests point a failure at the lowest broken layer ([Factorio](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-366)).

**Where it falls short.** Frostbite is the cautionary case. A shooter engine was mandated across genres, which pushed the missing pieces (save and load, a third-person camera, party members, inventory) onto each team. Central support followed projected revenue, and a lighting bake could take a day ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/how-biowares-anthem-went-wrong-1833731964); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-story-behind-mass-effect-andromedas-troubled-five-1795886428)). EA dropped the mandate in December 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frostbite_%28game_engine%29)). ECS storage pays off through native memory layout, which JavaScript does not offer. Separate version numbers for each lane would be false precision, because in a bit-exact simulation every observable behaviour is depended on, which is Hyrum's Law ([hyrumslaw.com](https://www.hyrumslaw.com/)). Abstractions built ahead of need pile up parameters and conditionals until they hurt ([Metz](https://sandimetz.com/blog/2016/1/20/the-wrong-abstraction)). Sightline's own weak spot is subtler. Order within a phase is deterministic today only because `SYSTEMS` is an array, which makes that order an unwritten contract between five labs.

**Beyond it.** Sightline can enforce from the start what others wrote down after years. Give every system `reads`, `writes` and `emits`, allow one writer per state subtree, and fail a test when two systems in one phase touch the same path without an explicit order. That is Bevy's ambiguity check, made deterministic and permanent. Write the lane map once, as data, and generate from it both the import rules for dependency-cruiser or eslint-plugin-boundaries ([dependency-cruiser](https://github.com/sverweij/dependency-cruiser)) and the architecture test's expectations. Give each lane one `index.ts`, with explicit exported types at its entry points, such as percepts and orders. Prove the kernel stands alone. A scene with every lane switched off should still step, hash, save, restore and replay, and two simulations stepped in alternation should keep their own hashes. That flushes out module-level state before the debrief replay or several bases need two worlds at once. Pin W0.3's spawn semantics now with a scene that checks on which tick a spawned body first exists for perception and for the motor. Overwatch had to add immediate creation in a stressful patch after release.

The same discipline extends to percepts, budgets and gates. Percepts become records with kind, strength, location, age and expiry, which is Unreal's perception contract ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ai-perception-in-unreal-engine)). Senses are set per body kind in tuning, so a future zombie can hear differently without new code. Budgets become deterministic counts of work per system, such as rays cast, grid nodes expanded and bodies resolved. They are pinned like goldens, and wall time is tracked only as a trend. Behaviour gates turn statistical. Once the camp brain is in the valley, accept changes to detection times and escalation outcomes by a sequential test across many seeds against the reference build. Stockfish does this with billions of games ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockfish_%28chess%29)), and a deterministic headless simulation makes it cheap. Generalize only at the third use ([Petrie](https://geometrian.com/programming/tutorials/write-games-not-engines/index.php)). "Camp" becomes "base" once the logging camp and comms relay exist. A framework for modes waits for a second mode. npm workspaces wait for a second consumer, slow checks or a boundary that has stopped moving. When those triggers arrive, two patterns are proven. Modes can come as patches over the base tuning, the way RimWorld's patch operations replaced whole-file overrides that silently discarded other mods' changes ([RimWorld wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Modding_Tutorials/PatchOperations)). Saves meant to outlive a build can migrate through a chain of small fixes keyed on `STATE_SCHEMA`, Mojang's DataFixerUpper pattern ([Mojang](https://github.com/Mojang/DataFixerUpper)). Recordings, meanwhile, stay pinned to the build that made them.

## Five cheap seams belong in Wave 0; the rest ride with the labs

The foundation plan's rule sets the timing: a seam goes in now only if it is cheap now and expensive to retrofit. Wave 0 items are the ones every lab needs before the branches diverge. Each lab item belongs to the lab that owns the system. Phase 2 items need the joined mission. Later items wait for doctrine, operations, a backend or a named trigger. Within each stage, the order is by value.

### Wave 0, before the first lab branch opens

| Rank | Add to Wave 0 | Why now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Goldens that explain themselves: CI prints the first divergent tick, the state path and meaningful numbers beside each moved fingerprint. ## Conclusion

The research changes the question Sightline should ask of prior art. Matching MGSV's adaptation, Hitman's sandbox or Riot's determinism is mostly done or already planned. What nobody has shipped are the things only a self-explaining simulation can do. These are readability tests scored against an executable answer key, a director proven honest by the same test the guards face, and a debrief that sets the player's knowledge beside the enemy's. Each falls out of rules Sightline already keeps (1, 5, 8, 9 and 13). In most lanes, then, reaching the top takes less new machinery than pointing existing machinery at a sharper question. The near-term risks are statistical and procedural rather than technical: an exit test that cannot cleanly separate a good reader from a lucky one, and five branches moving goldens by hand.

One design risk outranks the rest. Bungie's finding that sniping makes AI look oblivious comes closest, of anything in this evidence, to predicting how Sightline could fail. The game's core verb, a shot from 300 m, is exactly the moment when guards have the least time and the fewest pixels to show they noticed. The no-single-perch rule, the counter-sniper response and a visible reaction inside the 1.5 s window are therefore one system. Lab E should judge them together in the honest view before anything else is called proven. The starting numbers suggested throughout, such as slot weights, relax windows, thresholds and the 99.5% agreement bar, are hypotheses for the labs to tune, not findings.
