# Sniping balance and level design: stopping one perch from clearing the camp

Markers used below: **†** means I read the claim only in a search-result excerpt because the page itself would not open (403/402/truncated), so the exact wording is unverified. **(C)** means a community, wiki or strategy-guide source, which is lower confidence than a developer statement or a critic's review. Years in brackets are release years of the game the pattern shipped in.

## 1. How do sniper and stealth-action games stop a single vantage point from clearing a base? (A catalog by lever)

### Takeaway
No shipped game relies on one lever. The ones that hold up combine three things. First, an enemy that localizes repeated shots and moves on the perch. Second, escalation that is physical and can be interrupted (a runner, an alarm box, a radio). Third, a layout where something decisive, such as the target, a witness or the alarm, is out of any one perch's view part of the time. Where a lever was weak, players and critics report single-position clears as trivial: Sniper Elite's AI went back to routine, Far Cry's alarms could be shot from range, and MGSV's Quiet and Wildlands' sync shot did the spotting and killing for the player.

The reviewer called the third step magic and named the AI the game's biggest issue. — [Engadget/Joystiq review, Xav de Matos, 11 Jul 2014](https://www.engadget.com/2014-07-11-sniper-elite-3-review-war-and-pieces.html)

#### Lever 9: Terrain and vantage-point design

### Inferences
Each lever below gets a fit line for Sightline and a line on how it would work in a deterministic camp simulation where guards act only on what they perceived.

- **Shot localization.**
  - *Fit, strong:* SE4's model (the first shot starts an investigation, repeated shots from the same place converge on it) is the best-received form of the camp's existing "estimates its direction and sweeps likely perches". SE3's fixed third-shot reveal to everyone was called magic, and Arma's exact-position hearing is what modders set out to fix. Both are warnings against a fix that ignores who actually heard the shot.
  - *Deterministic sim:* each guard who perceives the report turns it into a bearing with error drawn from his own stream, `ctx.draw(guardId, 'shot-bearing', shotSeq)`. Bearings reach the commander by voice or radio through `ctx.schedule` with delays. The commander intersects them with `datan2`/`dsin`/`dcos` into a belief ellipse in `sim.state`, and repeated shots shrink it. Nothing reads the shooter's true position, which keeps rule 13.
- **Suppressor and crack.**
  - *Fit:* the Arma community distinction (the crack gives presence, the report gives direction) matches the design's suppressor rule. A suppressed supersonic shot should give camp-wide "we're under fire" and the side the bullet passed on, but no perch.
  - *Deterministic sim:* the event's type decides which belief fields a listener may update.
- **Counter-sniper marksmen.**
  - *Fit, strong but needs a telegraph:* SE5 shows only a human invader really pressures a perch, because the AI "can't sustain pressure". To come close, Sightline's marksmen should act like hunters: move to overlooking positions, scan candidate perches in order, and show glint. Battlefield's directional glint is the long-running proof that a visible telegraph keeps a counter-sniper fair.
  - *Deterministic sim:* the candidate perches are precomputed at generation in the site frame. The sweep visits them in a deterministic order ranked by the belief, with ties broken by perch id. Glint is a perception stimulus computed from the sun vector and the scope axis.
- **Pressure to relocate.**
  - *Fit:* make it emergent from the belief ellipse and the sweep, not a HUD meter. SE3's meter (move up to 30 m) was criticized and removed in SE4.
  - *Deterministic sim:* the belief decays as rate × `dt` (rule 17) and is anchored to the shot origin, so leaving the ellipse is the only thing that defeats the sweep.
- **Alarms and reinforcements.**
  - *Fit, strong with one caveat:* Far Cry and Wildlands let players disable alarms by shooting panels or generators from range, which is the main way a perch removes escalation altogether. If alarm boxes in Sightline can be shot from perches, the single overlook wins again. Alarm boxes (and the radio antenna) should be indoors, under eaves or otherwise unhittable from perches, so silencing escalation from afar means dropping the runner inside the reaction window, and sabotage needs a trip in.
  - *Deterministic sim:* the runner path-finds on the walk graph, and reaching the box is a state transition. The reinforcement truck's arrival and the radio check-ins are `ctx.schedule` events with their delays in `tuning.ts`.
- **Indoor objectives and exposure windows.**
  - *Fit, strong:* the Hitman Sniper Assassin and SGWC2 pattern ("learn when the targets will be out of the way") is exactly the planned "target visible at most 25% of the time from one perch".
  - *Deterministic sim:* routine is a pure function of time (`routine.ts`), so each perch's exposure of the target can be computed at generation by sampling the routine at fixed times, with an iteration budget (rule 18), and checked in validation.
- **Mutual view.**
  - *Fit, strong:* it is already the design's core (clusters plus a ~1.5 s reaction window), and it is the Shadow Tactics and Desperados model. Two warnings from Wildlands: tools that tag through walls, or sync several kills, collapse the mutual-view puzzle. Keep binocular marks to last-seen positions in the honest view.
  - *Deterministic sim:* the witness test is a perception query at the tick of the kill. The reaction is a scheduled event: 1.5 s from tuning plus jitter drawn from the witness's own stream.
- **Sound-masking windows.**
  - *Fit, with one condition:* the masking must be rare, periodic and legible, for example a generator's cycle, a convoy, or a plane on a timetable. SE3 had too little masking and SE4's was inconsistent. If masking is frequent, triangulation never starts and the single perch wins.
  - *Deterministic sim:* masking sources are scheduled entities in state. Masking is computed per listener as a loudness subtraction (`dlog`/`dexp`), so it applies near the source and not camp-wide, which matches the doc's "a guard next to a generator hears far less".
- **Limited ammunition.**
  - *Fit, weak against single-perch play:* no source shows ammo caps preventing perch camping in PvE, and Hunt's scarcity is about loadout variety. With about 15 rounds and 5–16 guards, ammo mainly limits shooting things (lights, generators, alarm boxes) and the margin for misses. It does not stop a clean single-perch kill of the target.
  - *Deterministic sim:* a counter in state set by the `MissionSpec`, so it is trivially deterministic.
- **The target's escape.**
  - *Fit, strong, but watch for Hitman's exploit:* there, players trigger the evacuation on purpose to bring targets into the open. If Sightline's "interceptable escape" route crosses the best overview perch, a camper causes Lockdown and shoots the target on the run. Make the escape visible only from perches other than the best overview perch, and fast or armoured (Wildlands' APC, C4 laid beforehand), so the player has to pre-position as part of the plan.
  - *Deterministic sim:* the escape is a routine-like path triggered by the Lockdown transition, so its exposure per perch is computable at generation.
- **Cross-mission doctrine.**
  - *Fit, strong:* MGSV's revenge system (helmets rising from 20% to 70%, enemy snipers for long-range habits, and dispatch missions that roll them back) is the closest shipped match to Sightline's doctrine table, and players accepted it as legible.
  - *Deterministic sim:* doctrine enters through the `MissionSpec`, so recordings replay it.

### Gaps
- No developer statement found on how Sniper Elite 4/5's triangulation works in numbers: how many shots before a fix, the error radius, or the decay. Only preview and review descriptions exist.
- No developer source for Sniper Ghost Warrior Contracts 1/2 alarm, mortar or counter-sniper rules. Reviews are inconsistent, and the PC Gamer review pages would not open.

## 2. Which level-design patterns limit what a single vantage point can do?

### Takeaway
The patterns developers state are these:
- Sniping positions that are themselves overlooked, and none fully covered (Sniper Elite: Resistance).
- Objectives spread over large maps (SE4).
- Compound buildings that give defenders cover (Hunt).
- Enemy marksmen on the highest points (Far Cry).
- Targets exposed only in time windows (Hitman: Sniper Assassin).
- Uniform enemy vision, so overlaps can be read (Shadow Tactics).

No studio publishes a coverage ratio. Sightline's "no single perch wins" rule would be more explicit than anything shipped.

### Inferences
These patterns translate into generator and validation checks Sightline could add next to the planned rule:
1. **A counter-perch for every perch.** Following SE: Resistance, each player perch is visible from at least one enemy marksman post, or from a ground position a counter-sniper team can reach within the search budget. No perch is fully covered. This makes counter-sniping geometric, not scripted.
2. **Interior share.** The target and intel spend most of the cycle occluded (indoors, under roofs or camo netting). Exposure comes in routine windows such as a walk between buildings, a smoke break or an inspection, so "at most 25% of the time from one perch" is designed in, not merely checked.
3. **Escalation out of reach.** No alarm box or antenna is hittable from a perch that also sees many guards. This addresses the Far Cry and Wildlands "shoot the panel from range" weakness.
4. **Perches spread around the compass, each seeing a different slice.** This extends the existing rule of perches in at least 3 of 8 sectors. Buildings and walls should be oriented so that the slices overlap little. Hunt's added defensive cover is the same idea.
5. **A relocation network.** Neighbouring perches are linked by dead-ground or reverse-slope paths, so relocating is a real option. SE4's "lure them to the sniping spot, then flank" needs these routes.
6. **Moving eyes on high ground.** At night, flares and lights do what Breakpoint's drones do (sweeping ridges). Shadow Tactics' rule that lights always create a bright zone suggests flares should fully expose a perch that is lit.
7. **A single fixed perch as an optional mode.** Hitman: Sniper Assassin shows one fixed perch can still be a good puzzle when found bodies trigger evacuation and targets have windows. A fixed-perch "contract" could be a deliberate mission type rather than an accident of generation.
8. **Define how coverage is counted.** "Sees at most 60% of the guards over the patrol cycle" can be read as a union over the cycle, in which case any patrol seen for a few seconds counts as seen, or as a time-average. The union reading lets a perch "see" almost every patroller. A threshold such as "visible for at least N s of the cycle" or a time-averaged share avoids that.

### Gaps
- No studio published a numeric rule for the share of the base visible from one vantage point, the spacing between perches, or relocation path lengths.
- No primary source covered the design rules of Far Cry or MGSV outposts. Only guides and paraphrased interviews exist.
- Tarkov map-design sources were too low quality to use.

### Inferences
- Across the series, perch dominance shows up under four conditions:
  - The camp forgets: beliefs decay to zero and guards return to routine beside bodies.
  - The player gets information he couldn't have: tags through walls, a companion who spots for him.
  - Escalation can be cut from range: alarm panels and generators can be shot.
  - The objective is exposed all the time.
- Sightline's design already addresses the first (suspicion decays to a raised baseline), and its honest view and fairness rules address the second, provided marks show last-seen positions only. The third and fourth need generator rules (section 2, items 2–3).

- Human counter-snipers (SE5's Invasion) are praised for pressure the AI can't sustain. Sightline's camp brain should give marksmen goal-directed hunting (move, flank, scan the believed perch), not static sector scans.

### Inferences
- **Search radius from bearing error.** At range R, a bearing error θ puts the believed perch about R·tan θ off to the side. At ±5° that is about 13 m at 150 m, 26 m at 300 m and 44 m at 500 m. At ±10° it is about 26 m, 53 m and 88 m. A ±5° listener error at Sightline's typical 300 m gives roughly SE3's 30 m relocation distance, so the belief ellipse would produce "relocate about 30 m" without a meter, and further at longer range.
- **Range from the crack.** The crack-to-report interval gives distance: about 0.5 s at 300 m for an 800 m/s bullet, from 300/343 − 300/800. The design doc's "little sense of distance" can be kept by having untrained guards ignore it, or used as a marksman skill. A suppressed shot gives no interval at all.
- **Chaining inside the reaction window.** With a 1.5 s window and about 0.4 s of flight to 300 m, chaining a second shot requires re-aiming and cycling the bolt in about 1.5 s from the first trigger pull. Whether a skilled player can do it depends on lab B's bolt time. A cluster of three in mutual view should be beyond what one bolt-action perch can chain, so it needs a split, a distraction or a masking window. This would make "core clusters" the structural counter to the single perch.
- **Ammo versus guards.** About 15 rounds against 5–16 guards means ammo binds only when the player also shoots equipment or misses. It is a weak lever on its own.
- **Search duration.** SE5's "a few minutes, then back to posts" matches the doc's "searches end after a time budget". Critics' complaints about guards forgetting support settling one level higher.

- No published value for how fast AI moves on a shooter's position, how far it searches after a shot (beyond SE3's 30 m), or how long relocation should take.
- No published share-of-guards-visible threshold in any game.
- Vanilla Arma 3 and Reforger hearing figures are known only through mod pages.

## 5. What does this imply for Sightline's levers and its "no single perch wins" rule?

### Takeaway
Sightline's planned levers (counter-sniper marksmen and glint, shot direction, sweeps, the 1.5 s window, check-ins, the target's evacuation, about 15 rounds, mutual-view clusters) cover every lever that worked in shipped games. Three gaps from prior art matter most:
- Escalation that can be cut from the perch, such as alarm boxes shootable from range.
- Tagging that leaks information through walls.
- An escape route that passes the best overview perch.

The planned validation rule is also stricter and more explicit than anything shipped, so its counting method needs pinning down.

### Cited Findings
- **The design's current validation rule:** "long range: at least two perches that see half the guards and the objective", and "an interceptable escape: the target's escape route crosses a perch's sightline or passes a sabotage point." — [Sightline design doc, Maps and procedural camps](/Users/gibby/local/ai/sightline/docs/design-doc.md)
- **Escalation and counter-sniping as designed:** escalating to Lockdown is physical (an alarm box or a working radio), and after an unsuppressed shot the camp estimates direction and sweeps likely perches with marksmen, flares or smoke. — [Sightline design doc, Enemy awareness and the camp brain](/Users/gibby/local/ai/sightline/docs/design-doc.md)
- **Doctrine:** if the player leans on one perch, the faction adopts counter-sniper teams and smoke and flares on suspected perches, announced in the briefing, with at most three active at once. — [Sightline design doc, Adaptation](/Users/gibby/local/ai/sightline/docs/design-doc.md)
- **Hearing:** a heard shot gives a guess, not a fix, and a shot from 300 m gives a rough direction and little sense of distance. — [Sightline design doc, Hearing](/Users/gibby/local/ai/sightline/docs/design-doc.md)
- **Prior art on perches that are themselves overlooked:** each sniping spot visible from two or three others (SE: Resistance). — [Epic Games Store interview](https://store.epicgames.com/news/sniper-elite-resistance-interview-going-even-deeper-behind-enemy-lines)†
- **Prior art on alarms disabled from range:** Far Cry 3 lets you shoot alarms (GamerGuides (C)†), and Wildlands lets you shoot alarm panels with a sniper rifle (Prima (C)†). — [GamerGuides](https://www.gamerguides.com/far-cry-3/introduction/gameplay/outposts); [Prima](https://primagames.com/eguides/tom-clancys-ghost-recon-wildlands-eguide/welcome-to-bolivia/special-missions/el-muro)

### Inferences
1. **Reconcile the two coverage rules.** The current "at least two perches see half the guards and the objective" and the planned "no perch sees more than about 60% of guards, or the target more than 25% of the time" together define a narrow band: the best perches see 50–60% of guards, and the target in windows totalling at most 25% of the cycle. Both should use one counting method. A time-averaged share, or "visible for at least N s", is better than a union over the cycle (section 2, item 8). "Sees the objective" should then mean "has at least one exposure window", not "sees it continuously".
2. **Add a counter-perch rule** (from SE: Resistance). Each perch is overlooked by at least one marksman post or one reachable counter-perch. Together with "at least one visible guard is watched by an unseen one", this makes the camp's answer to a perch geometric and explainable in the debug view, which rule 8 requires.
3. **Make escalation unreachable from perches.** Alarm boxes and the antenna get placement constraints: no line of fire from any perch that sees at least X% of guards. Silencing the alarm from the perch then means dropping the runner inside the window. Dropping the runner is already "a real play" in the design, and it rewards timing, not a free shot.
4. **Build triangulation as beliefs.** Per-listener bearings with seeded error are fused by the commander over radio, with delay, into an ellipse that shrinks with repeated shots and decays as rate × `dt`. This reproduces SE4's well-received behaviour, avoids SE3's "magic" and Arma's exact fix, and gives relocation pressure without a meter. Everything stays inside rules 2, 3, 12, 13 and 17: per-entity draws, `ctx.schedule` for radio delays, `detmath` for the geometry, and beliefs in `sim.state`.
5. **Give the sweep a telegraph and an order.** Marksmen visit candidate perches in a deterministic order by belief probability, with visible glint and flares. That turns "shoot, then relocate" into a readable timing puzzle (Battlefield's glint, MGS3's The End).
6. **Use masking sparingly and predictably.** Periodic, local masking (a generator cycle, a convoy, a plane timetable) gives the planned "chained or timed shots" a window without letting every shot go untriangulated (SE3 and SE4 criticism).
7. **Keep marks honest.** Binocular marks show last-seen positions and never track through walls, so the mutual-view puzzle survives (Wildlands' sync-shot and wall complaints, MGSV's Quiet).
8. **Watch the evacuation exploit.** Validate that the escape route is interceptable only from perches other than the best overview perch, so planning (pre-positioning, sabotaging the vehicle) beats camping (Hitman's deliberate evacuation, Wildlands' El Muro).
9. **Treat ammo as a secondary lever.** About 15 rounds shapes shooting equipment and the margin for misses, not single-perch play. Decoy dummies at posts, as in MGSV's covert preparedness, would make ammo and position-revealing shots matter together. This is optional and a taste call for the owner.
10. **Measure these in the labs.** Lab E (camp brain) can test the ellipse, the sweep timing and escalation. Lab D (generator) can add counter-perch, interior-share and alarm-reach checks to validation. Lab B (shooting) sets the bolt time that decides whether pairs or only triples defeat a single perch.

### Gaps
- No prior art gives numbers for the right relocation budget, sweep speed or belief decay. They have to be tuned in lab E.
- No playtest data shows whether a 25% target-exposure cap feels fair or frustrating. Hitman: Sniper Assassin's 15-minute limit with three targets is the only comparable published constraint.
